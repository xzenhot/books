## creative_enrichment

### angles

- Framing the hunger not as a need for *recognition*, but as an ontological requirement—the inability to cease existing outside of creation itself.
- Viewing the 'hunger' through the lens of tradition: contrasting Debolina’s raw, untamed hunger against established cultural expectations for female devotion or domestic fulfillment in Shekhawati society.
- The hunger expressed as a physical ache rather than purely emotional longing; where the body yearns for the act of making pigment/lime itself.

### subject_rotations

- Treating 'hunger' as a historical/economic force—the relentless drive required by the twilight of merchant wealth.
- Reframing the hunger relationally: how does her need for existence alter her perception of the other living beings in Mandawa?
- Focusing on the *negation* inherent in the hunger—what must she actively destroy or reject (comfort, security) to sustain this craving?

- The texture of strained muscle memory in hands habitually positioned for brushstrokes during deep sleep-hours.
- The specific resonance frequency of a single drop of liquid pigment hitting dry, porous plaster.
