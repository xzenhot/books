To write a compelling novel about *Shekhawat Haveli*, you can populate its century-old corridors with different layers of the supernatural. In Indian folklore, not all spirits are the same; they are shaped by how they died, what they desired, and how long they have been left to rot in the dark. 

Here is a fictional breakdown of the four distinct entities that inhabit the haveli, each serving a different psychological and narrative function for your story:

---

### 1. The *Atma* (The Pure, Trapped Essence): **The Diwan’s Blind Conscience**
* **Who they were:** Diwan Hukumchand, the aging, blind administrator of the haveli who tried to warn the patriarch against sealing the vault in 1947, and was walled in alongside the victims for his defiance.
* **Nature of the Spirit:** An *atma* is a soul caught in a state of suspended animation. Hukumchand’s spirit is tied to the physical structure of the haveli itself. He does not terrify; he bears witness. 
* **In the Novel:** He manifests as the sound of a slow, dragging wooden cane tapping against the marble upper balconies, always pacing just out of sight. When Adhiraj walks the halls at night, a sudden drop in temperature and the smell of old parchment and dry ink signify the *atma’s* presence. He leaves cryptic messages written in dust on windowpanes, pointing Adhiraj toward the truth. He represents **guilt and moral memory**.

### 2. The *Bhoot* (The Historical Echo): **The Greedy Patriarch**
* **Who they were:** Rai Bahadur Pratap Singh Shekhawat, the master of the house who died clawing his own throat in a fit of absolute madness.
* **Nature of the Spirit:** A *bhoot* is a standard human spirit tethered to earth by sudden, violent death and supreme unresolved ego. Pratap Singh doesn’t realize—or refuses to accept—that he is dead. He believes the year is still 1947, and that intruders (like Adhiraj) are trying to steal his hidden wealth.
* **In the Novel:** The *bhoot* is the primary source of active terror. He manipulates the physical environment—slamming heavy teak doors, locking windows, and manifesting as a towering, imposing shadow wearing a traditional *achkan* and royal turban. When Adhiraj looks into the convex mirrors of the Sheesh Mahal, he doesn't see his own reflection; he sees Pratap’s furious, bloodshot eyes glaring back from a century ago. He represents **greed and generational trauma**.

### 3. The *Petni* (The Twisted Vengeance): **The Abandonded Bride**
* **Who they were:** Chandrakantha, a young royal bride from a neighboring estate who was brought to the haveli days before the Partition, only for her groom to vanish into the riots. She died of grief and neglect in the locked guest quarters, her wedding finery turning to rags around her skeleton.
* **Nature of the Spirit:** In folklore, a *petni* is the restless, feral spirit of a woman who died with intense, unfulfilled earthly and marital desires. Over a century, her sorrow curdled into a mocking, erratic hunger for life.
* **In the Novel:** The *petni* haunts the inner courtyards and the neglected gardens where the dry bougainvillea grows. She appears as a flickering silhouette of a woman in a shredded red bridal *lehenga*. Her feet are turned backward—the classic mark of her unnatural state. She lures Adhiraj with the faint sound of weeping and chiming silver anklets (*ghungroos*), trying to trick him into following her into rooms where the floors have rotted through. She represents **sorrow twisted into malice**.

### 4. The *Chudel* / Dark Entity of the Vault: **The Collective Wrath**
* **Who they were:** Not a single person, but a collective egregore—the concentrated, fused agony of the dozens of merchants and refugees starved in the subterranean *tehkhana*. 
* **Nature of the Spirit:** Over a hundred years of shared suffocation in pitch-black darkness, their individual identities melted into a singular, predatory force that guards the basement vault. 
* **In the Novel:** This entity is never fully seen, only felt as a heavy, suffocating pressure in the air—a multitude of breathing mouths whispering simultaneously in the dark. It feeds on fear. When Adhiraj tries to descend into the cellar, it is this entity that pushes him back up the stairs with invisible, violent hands, scratching deep, bleeding parallel lines into his forearms. It represents **retributive justice and the inescapable weight of the past**.

---

### How to Use Them Together in Your Plot:
* **The Escalation:** When Adhiraj first arrives, he only hears the gentle, tragic tapping of the **Atma** (Hukumchand). 
* **The Illusion:** As he stays longer, the **Petni** (Chandrakantha) tries to lead him astray into physical danger.
* **The Confrontation:** The **Bhoot** (Pratap Singh) actively tries to drive him insane or lock him inside the haveli forever.
* **The Climax:** To survive, Adhiraj must brave the basement guarded by the **Chudel** collective, using the historical clues left by the Atma to finally lay all their tormented souls to rest.
