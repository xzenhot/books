# Research Note — Observation of Small Things in Daily Social Life in India

## Subject

This book observes the small, overlooked particulars of everyday social life in India and lets each stand for a larger truth about ritual, labor, class, and belonging. It is a poetry sequence grounded in the concrete texture of contemporary Indian daily life rather than in a single historical event or named figure.

## Scope of the gist

The gist names no single place, person, or date. It names a mode of attention: the tiny, half-seen gestures and objects of daily social life. The concrete particulars the sequence draws on (chai, vermilion, folded hands, temple slippers, tiffin, kolam, paan, marigold, the monsoon) are drawn from pan-Indian everyday practice and are used as images, not as factual claims.

## What is grounded vs. what must be researched

- **Grounded in common practice (low risk):** the chai glass on a railway platform, the vermilion (sindoor) at a doorway, folded hands (namaste) as greeting, slippers left outside a temple, the tiffin box, marigold garlands at flower stalls, the kolam/rangoli drawn at dawn, the betel leaf (paan), the monsoon's first rain on warm dust.
- **Needs care (regional variation):** the kolam is South Indian (rangoli in the North; alpana in Bengal); the ration shop queue varies by state; the school uniform and the rickshaw bell are urban/regional particulars. Flag these rather than asserting a single national form.
- **Avoid asserting:** any single "Indian" ritual as universal — the sequence should allow for regional diversity rather than flatten it.

## Notes for the writer/research pass

- Prefer concrete, sensory imagery over abstraction; the particular is the proof.
- Where a practice is regional (kolam vs. alpana vs. rangoli), choose the image carefully and let it carry its own locality.
- The poems observe; they do not judge. Keep the tone attentive and tender, not sociological.

## Remaining to research

- Confirm any regional variants of each image before the correctness filter runs.
- If a poem names a specific place or institution, ground it with a real reference rather than a generic one.
