# Research Note — লক্ষ্মণ: গঙ্গার যুদ্ধে কনৌজ

This note summarizes the book's subject as stated in the gist, for grounding by later writer/research passes. Flagged claims are marked where historical certainty is limited.

## Subject overview

The gist names four interlocking subjects:

1. **বাংলার গৌরব (Bengal's glory)** — the cultural and political prestige of Bengal as a distinct regional identity.
2. **সেন যুগের রাজনীতি (the politics of the Sena era)** — the Sena dynasty of Bengal (c. 11th–12th century CE), its court, its administration, and its social-religious institutions.
3. **দিল্লির বাঙালি যাত্রামঞ্চ (the Bengali yatra stage in Delhi)** — a modern framing device: a Bengali folk-theatre troupe among the expatriate Bengali community of Delhi.
4. **যুবরাজ লক্ষ্মণ সেনের কনৌজ-অভিযান (Prince Lakshmana Sena's Kanauj campaign)** — the central dramatic event, framed as a Gangetic military expedition toward Kanauj.

## Historical grounding (the Sena dynasty)

- **Vijaya Sena** (c. 1095–1158 CE): founder of Sena power; rose after the decline of the Pala and Varman dynasties; established his base around Vikrampur in eastern Bengal.
- **Ballala Sena** (r. c. 1158–1179 CE): son of Vijaya Sena; a patron of learning traditionally credited with composing the *Dana Sagara* and *Adbhuta Sagara* (a treatise on omens/portents). He is associated with the introduction/consolidation of the **kulin** (kulinism) system of Brahmin and Kayastha social hierarchy.
- **Lakshmana Sena** (r. c. 1179–1206 CE): son of Ballala Sena; the last major Sena ruler. His reign ended amid the early Ghurid/Turkic incursions (the conquests often associated with Bakhtiyar Khalji).
- The Sena court hosted poets such as **Jayadeva** (author of the *Gita Govinda*) and **Umāpatidhara** and **Halāyudha** (or a Halāyudha associated with Brahminical works).

*Uncertain/flagged:* A specific "Kanauj campaign" by Lakshmana Sena is a **fictional premise**, not an established historical event. Kanauj's dominance had already declined after the Ghaznavid raids (11th century) and the Gāhaḍavāla dynasty (centered at Kanauj) was itself disrupted by the late 12th-century Ghurid conquests. The novel treats this as imaginative history.

## Contextual threads named by the gist

- **The Gangetic river economy** — Bengal's river-based trade, agriculture, waterways, and granaries as the material basis of power.
- **Nalanda** — the great Buddhist monastery, already in decline/ruin by this era; its memory as a symbol of destroyed knowledge.
- **The Doab** — the Ganges–Yamuna region; unfamiliar terrain, culture, and warfare for Bengal's soldiers.
- **Khajuraho / Chandela** — the Chandela rulers and regional powers around Khajuraho; the "Kalachuri/Carnatic" and Rajput alliances.
- **Religion and power** — Brahminical orthodoxy, Buddhist memory, folk religion, and (in the later period) new faiths coexisting and competing.

## Remaining to research

- The Gāhaḍavāla kingdom of Kanauj at the end of the 12th century.
