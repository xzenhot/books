## Subject

The gist grounds the sequence in the majhi (boatman) of rural Bengal, his small wooden boat (nouka), the rivers of Bengal that built the delta, the Bhatiyali folk songs of the boatmen, and a tradition of generosity that ferries even those who cannot pay. The expansion names a specific late-1970s setting on the Ganges tributaries and the arrival of modernization (dredging, diesel engines, concrete cities).

## Research cautions

- Treat "the rivers that made the land" as the delta-building work of silt deposition; do not assert a specific geological date or a single river's priority.
- Bhatiyali is a real boatmen's song tradition of Bengal; keep its sorrow-and-sweetness register as imagery, not as a musicological claim.
- Do not invent named historical persons, specific dredging projects, or dates. The engineer "Mr. Chatterjee" is a character, not a documented figure.
- The late-1970s setting is the gist's own frame; do not add political or infrastructural specifics beyond it.

## Remaining to research

- The geography and names of the Bengal delta's rivers and tributaries.
- The Bhatiyali tradition: its themes, register, and association with boatmen.
- The social economy of river ferrying and the tradition of free passage for the poor.
- The impact of dredging and motorized transport on Bengal's inland waterways.
