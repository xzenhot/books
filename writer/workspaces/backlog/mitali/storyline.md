## Premise

A cycle of forty poems, one for each friend, remembering a school in a Bengal village where forty children — twenty boys and twenty girls — once shared their days. The poems are spoken by one who calls "Friend, where are you?" into the years that scattered them, and who answers by returning to the small, freely-offered things of childhood: a shared tiffin, a monsoon walk, a song under a tree, a hand held at the edge of the field. Each poem opens one human emotion and lets it surface through a single small object or gesture, so that the whole cycle becomes a roll-call of feelings — joy, sorrow, longing, courage, envy, forgiveness, tenderness, faith, regret, peace — revealed in the plain texture of village life.

## Setting & Era

The poems live in a Bengal village of the school years — the classroom with its wooden benches, the field and the river, the tiffin hour, the monsoon that turns paths to mud, the banyan and the neem, the tube-well, the lantern-lit evening, the pond, the paddy. Time moves between that remembered childhood and the scattered present, where the forty have gone to cities and different lives; the poems reach back across that distance, letting each small thing of the village become a doorway to a feeling that still waits to be spoken.

## Characters

The forty friends are the characters — twenty boys and twenty girls, each remembered through a single emotion and a single small thing. The speaker is one among them, the voice that calls and remembers. Beyond the forty, the village itself is a presence: the teacher, the mother who packed the tiffin, the playmate, the beloved, the rival, the one who left early and never returned.

## Thematic Threads

- **The talk of the mind** — a life is not a life without words shared; silence is the first loss.
- **The shared feeling** — the same emotion, known without being said, binding the forty across distance.
- **The simple touch** — a hand, a shoulder, a passing brush; the body remembers what the years cannot take.
- **Nostalgia as revelation** — the small things of the village are not small; they carry the whole weight of a feeling.
- **The roll-call of emotions** — forty friends, forty feelings; the cycle maps the human heart one name at a time.

## Topical Structure

Forty poems, one per friend, ordered as a call-and-response of emotions: the bright courage of first friendship, the quiet ache of longing, the generosity of the shared tiffin, the joy of play, the tenderness of a first touch, the envy and forgiveness between rivals, the faith of a promise, the grief of the one who left, and at last the peace of reconnection — the talk of the mind and the touch of the hand made whole again.

## Conclusion

The cycle ends not in reunion but in the act of remembering — the forty names spoken aloud, each feeling named and so made bearable. The final poem returns to the village at dusk, where the small things still wait: the bench, the lantern, the path to the pond — and the call "Friend, where are you?" becomes, at last, its own answer.
