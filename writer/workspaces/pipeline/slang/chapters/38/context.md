## chapter_summary

The chapter delves into the stark reality of a certain 'chhamra' (loafer) who occupies the streets, embodying a profound sense of aimlessness and characterlessness. This figure serves as a living metaphor for societal stagnation, his very presence a silent critique of lost potential in the bustling urban landscape. The imagery is dominated by the gritty textures of the pavement and the indifferent flow of the city around him, contrasting sharply with the weight of his unfulfilled existence. As the narrative observes this tableau of idle life, the central question hangs in the air: Can such pervasive apathy ever be broken, or is this state of passive existence an inescapable fate for those adrift in the modern city?

- Analyzing the protagonist's inaction through a purely economic lens: his state is a form of passive labor optimized for minimal input and exposure.
- Treating the city itself as the active antagonist; the streets are not just the setting but a force that actively drains energy from idle figures like him.
