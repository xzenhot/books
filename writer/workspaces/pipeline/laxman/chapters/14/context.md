### angles

- Framing the entire scene not through Lakshman's epic gaze, but from Malini’s perspective—the logistical headache of accommodating an unstoppable military machine against her village's precarious subsistence needs.
- Treating the 'cost' Ledger as a micro-economic audit: tallying grains lost versus ritualistic favor gained by providing passage to the army convoy.
- Focusing on the invisible labor exchange: how the promise of future political favor (glory) is traded against immediate, quantifiable suffering (loss of harvest/labor capacity).

- Exploring the dissonance between the grand martial narrative (victory) and the mundane, cyclical reality of village resource depletion.
