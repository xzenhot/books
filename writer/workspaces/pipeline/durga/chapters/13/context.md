## creative_enrichment

### angles

- Treating the 'বোধন' not as an awakening to divinity, but as a necessary shedding of inert matter—the clay must be momentarily humbled before it can receive divine fire.
- Focusing on the subtle shift in auditory texture: moving from the mundane sounds of preparation (a child crying, grinding paste) to the sudden, formal resonance of the first ritual chant.
- Structuring the poem around a single, persistent light source—perhaps an oil lamp placed near the idol base—and tracking how that flickering light alters the perceived solidity and form of the deity throughout the evening.

### subject_rotations

- Focusing on 'the invitation' itself: not what is being called, but the act of calling—the human vulnerability inherent in the summoning.
- Reversing the focus: treating the clay idol as a temporary receptacle, and the ritual chants as external forces that momentarily animate it from without.
- Exploring the micro-politics of devotion: contrasting the fervent internal prayer of one devotee with the formal, societal performance of worship at this specific junction.

- An opening that begins *after* the main event (the awakening), using retrospective imagery to establish the gravity of the moment before unraveling backward toward the preparatory actions.
