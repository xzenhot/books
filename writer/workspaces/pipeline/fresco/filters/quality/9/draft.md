The sun, a blunt anvil striking the ochre-stained courtyard, finally hammered down upon the finished skin, and the town inhaled. A chorus rose, a sound thick with the sweet, immediate validation of the bloom—the peacock's tail catching the light, the vine’s geometry praised by tongues that knew nothing of the scaffold's ache, the night’s relentless, lime-powdered vigil. Dust, stirred by the sudden influx of appreciative breath, settled slowly, a fine, indifferent shroud across the meticulously laid lapis. The praise circled the motif, the brilliant, arrested curve of a painted wing, never quite reaching the edge where the artist stood, a shadow etched against the finished narrative. Amidst the bright, loud affirmation, one figure paused—the merchant’s wife, perhaps, or the daughter of a distant landowner—and her gaze did not trace the vibrant blue, nor the deep vermilion. It settled, instead, on the sheer, unburdened surface, as if reading the substrate itself, a sudden, profound absorption that held the weight of the prior failures, the erased sketches beneath the curing wash. Then, with a breath barely disturbing the scent of sun-baked plaster and cooling pigment, she turned, melting back into the tide of admirers, dissolving into the pattern of the crowd as if startled by the wall’s own sudden, complete silence.
