The metallic taste of crushed lapis, sharp and dry, coats the tongue, a residue of nights spent breathing the powdered confession of the earth. Under the scaffold, where the bone-frame of the wall rises against the indifferent smear of pre-dawn, the final stroke hesitated. It was not the pigment that stalled the hand—the binding oil was still tacky, clinging to the brush like cooled blood—but the weight of the unwritten testament. To name the tremor, to articulate the grinding friction between the desire for the flawless bloom and the reality of the aching shoulder, felt like a trespass against the silent covenant of the haveli. He could speak, could let the litany of labor fall against the damp plaster, a sudden, sharp sound swallowed by the vast, sleeping courtyard. But the breath caught, held taut by the sub-sonic hum of the aged timber against the cool desert air. The town stirred, a slow, grey exhale before the sun, and the decision crystallized: no declaration. He lowered the brush, not to wipe, but to rest it against the rough lime, a deliberate pause. The struggle remained unparsed, unarticulated, absorbed back into the porous substrate. And as the first pale wash of light found the curve of the finished niche, illuminating the perfect, vibrant lie, he remained a shadow against the rough, unpainted edge of the scaffold, his own name a syllable never allowed to settle onto the wall.
