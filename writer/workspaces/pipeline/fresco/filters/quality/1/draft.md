The lime-plastered expanse, bone-white under the bruised pre-dawn, held the silence of a thousand dry monsoons, a sedimentary record of sun and slow decay. He stood before it, the scaffolding a skeleton against the sleeping ochre of the haveli courtyard, the chalk dust clinging to his skin like fine, powdered mortality. It was not a canvas; it was the boundary marker between the dreaming interior and the hungry, dust-breathing world outside. Under the cold, metallic tang of the rising air, his gaze did not search for a subject, but for the deep, unseen fissures in the lower courses—those subtle cracks whispering of foundation, mirroring the uncertain pact he held with the coming day. He felt the weight of every un-painted possibility, the terrifying scope of what might erupt from this blankness: the peacock’s sudden flight, the bride’s hesitant grace, the deity’s silent blessing. His hand, stained faintly with the ghost of yesterday's lapis, hovered, tracing an imaginary sigil onto the receptive surface, a silent acknowledgment of the debt owed to the ancestors who built these enduring shells. Then, a distant, tinny chime, perhaps from a distant temple, fractured the vigil. The charcoal slipped, a dark, brittle leaf falling onto the waiting, immaculate skin of the wall.
