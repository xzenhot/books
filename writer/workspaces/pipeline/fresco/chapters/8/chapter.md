The tallow lamp fought the encroaching dark, a solitary, sweating eye against the vast, indifferent stone of the courtyard, and in that fragile circle of light, the grinding began. The pestle kissed the lapis, a sound like brittle, ancient music—a high, persistent friction against the cool, unforgiving grindstone. Each turn was a small surrender, the geological patience of distant mountains being violently reduced, particle by particle, into something momentarily usable. The air thickened, not with the sweet scent of jasmine, but with the dry, mineral breath of crushed rock, a sharp perfume of labor and distant trade routes. He watched the blue bleed from the stone, not as a hue, but as a physical exhaustion, the deep, cold permanence of the ore surrendering its weight into a fine, shimmering dust. This dust, this powder, was not mere color; it was banked nights, the toll exacted by the scarcity of the mountain and the silence of the sleeping town. He held the small, heavy pile up to the sputtering flame, seeking the truth in the shadow-dance, wondering if this concentrated sacrifice, this blue bought with hours bled dry under the scaffold’s shadow, would truly capture the myth he sought, or if the lamplight would only distort it into a beautiful, temporary lie.
