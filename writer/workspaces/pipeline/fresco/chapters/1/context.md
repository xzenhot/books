### angles

- Framing Debolina’s initial vision through the lens of a ruined pigment pile—the raw material before it becomes an image.
- Focusing on the internal conflict between the 'self' she wants to create and the societal expectations represented by absence (an empty domestic space).
- Using the concept of negative space as the first artistic act, equating the void with ultimate form.

### story_options

- In a half-empty haveli morning, Debolina sees a sliver of sun hitting bare plaster; she sketches the line of that light, desiring immediate purity but blocked by her current tools.
- A memory flash: seeing an idealized depiction of a woman in one of the havelis—the finished product—and consciously rejecting its softness for hard geometry.
- She attempts to mix color pigments under natural daylight, finding them too diluted and mutable; the desire is for the permanence only night-bound chemical reactions promise.

### subject_rotations

- Treating 'blank canvas' not as an opportunity but as a confrontation with silence itself.
- Examining how her minimalist pursuit mirrors the severe, uncompromising architecture of the havelis before any paint is applied.
- Focusing on the *act* of subtraction—what must be removed from perception to arrive at the essential line.
