The grinding stone, slicked with the sweat of the long night, held the dust of sorrow; lapis, powdered down until it whispered of crushed midnight. Each rotation of the pestle was a slow, deliberate confession, a translation of the unattainable blue into something that could cling, however briefly, to the yielding bone of the lime plaster. The forearm ached, a dull, rhythmic protest against the mountain of pigment, the mineral weight of the earth insisting on its own memory. He sought the exact saturation of that high summer sunset, a hue glimpsed once between the dust-haze and the evening’s sudden descent, but the grind was flawed—a microscopic grit caught in the mortar, dulling the vibrant promise. To discard it was to admit failure to the silent courtyard, to let the exquisite yearning settle into inert powder. Yet, to incorporate the imperfection, to let that minute shadow of flawed grit become part of the matrix, was to surrender the ideal to the truth of the hand that held the pestle. The slurry, cool and silken, smeared over the skin, binding the heavy, earthen pride of the ochres with the cool, desperate yearning of the blue. A sudden gust, thin and indifferent, swept through the half-empty *haveli*, dusting the pale, waiting expanse of the wall. He did not wipe the chalk from the damp base; instead, he watched the white, the substrate, accept the intrusion, knowing that the struggle to preserve the perfect blankness was itself a pigment, a history etched in the slow, porous breath of the mortar.
