The scaffold rail, cold iron against the pre-dawn chill, held the tremor—a faint, involuntary vibration running up the wrist that the finished lapis blue would never confess. It was not the pigment that demanded perfection, but the bone structure beneath the stained cotton, the muscle memory straining against the gravity of the high wall. Dust, the fine, ochre residue of a thousand nights, clung to the skin like a brittle patina, mapping the contours of unseen labor onto the landscape of the hand. He traced a line—a meticulous drag of the stiff bristle brush against the already set lime—and felt the resistance, the subtle, microscopic imperfection where the binder had hesitated, a betrayal caught in the substrate. This minute flaw, invisible to the merchant whose gaze would only register the bloom, was the true confession. He saw the landscape of his own geography mapped onto the fresco, the valleys of fatigue etched around the knuckles, the ridges of focused pressure. He did not wipe the imperfection away; he let the raw, dusty edge of the brush linger there, a deliberate, silent stain against the intended serenity, leaving the wall to hold the secret weight of the hand that shaped it.
