The scent of burnt charcoal and linseed oil, sharp and acrid, hung heavy beneath the scaffold's skeletal shadow, a graveyard breath exhaled by the night. Here, piled like brittle, discarded constellations, lay the wasted sketches—the preliminary breath before the wall could hold the pigment. Each curled page, a topographical map of a thought abandoned; spirals that tightened into nothing, grids that stuttered mid-line, the ghost of a clandestine meeting glimpsed only in a smudge of charcoal near the corner, half-swallowed by the damp bloom of mold. He traced the outline with a thumb hardened by lime dust, the paper yielding not to the pressure of intention, but to the slow, insistent erasure of time, the insect trails charting a private history deeper than any fresco layer. These were not failures, he felt, watching the moonlight catch the fine hairs on his forearms as he leaned over the heap; they were potential energy, compressed, unspent charge. A future self, perhaps, would later impose a meaning upon this accretion of near-things, a grand narrative woven from these hesitant gestures. But for now, they were only the negative space, the vast, necessary absence that defined the single, stubborn leaf of the blossom that had finally bloomed on the finished facade. The tower of fragile paper, stacked against the cold, indifferent stone, shuddered, then surrendered to the vibration of a distant, sleeping village animal, collapsing into a heap of brown silence.
