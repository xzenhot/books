# The Failure

The failure happens at the worst possible hour, when the wall is half done and the morning is only a few lamps away. A line goes wrong — a face that will not hold its expression, a hand that points where no hand should point — and the painter steps back and sees, with the cold clarity that only failure brings, that the whole panel is ruined.

He could leave it. No one would catch him at it in this light; the town would wake and see the wall nearly finished and praise it, and no one would notice the one wrong face among the right ones. But he would notice. He would see it every day for the rest of his life, the way a man sees the crack in his own ceiling. So he does what the work has taught him to do: he takes up the trowel and strips the plaster, and begins again.

It is the worst labor there is, undoing. The wet plaster comes away in long gray sheets, taking the ruined face with it, and the wall beneath is blank again, as if the hours had never happened. But the hours did happen, and they are not lost — they are buried, now, under the fresh coat, the failed face sleeping beneath the new one like a secret the wall will keep. He paints over it and goes on. And he understands, scraping and smoothing, that every finished surface is a palimpsest, and that a thing can be whole even when its buried errors still breathe beneath it.
