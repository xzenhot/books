## Canonical gist

Of all the subjects poetry has ever loved, none has held the imagination like the moon — and this book is one poet's long, unfinished devotion to the moon and to moonlight.

## Expansion

The moon is the oldest companion of poets: it keeps their sleepless hours, walks with them on roads they did not choose, and returns each night in a slightly different shape without ever leaving. This fascination is not astronomy but intimacy — moonlight falling on water, on a rooftop, on a face turned away, turning ordinary longing into something shared and almost sacred. The poems move through the moon's phases as the phases of love and memory: the thin crescent of first wonder, the swollen full moon of belonging, the slow waning of distance, and the dark nights when only the memory of light remains. Throughout, the moon stays beloved and unreachable at once — close enough to be addressed, far enough to remain a question.
