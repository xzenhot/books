## creative_enrichment

### angles

- Focusing entirely on the *sound* profile of the oil—the sharp 'splatter' versus the muted absorption in foreign cooking.
- Framing the oil not as a culinary choice, but as a silent negotiation between necessity (survival) and devotion (memory).
- Adopting the perspective of the inanimate vessel (*কড়াই/tawa*) that holds both types of oils, witnessing the transition.

### story_options

- Setting: A half-empty packet of neutral oil on a counter; Desire: To trace the geometric patterns left by the old mustard oil stains; Obstacle: The sharp scent of manufactured detergent overpowering all residual memory.
- Setting: Beside an open border road where two types of cooking gas burners are visible; Desire: To juxtapose the flicker of the 'আঁচ' in both settings; Obstacle: The constant, indifferent hum of foreign machinery drowning out intimate sensory detail.
- Setting: While polishing a traditional *tawa* to use it for the new oil; Desire: To feel the residual heat memory on the metal surface despite the coldness; Obstacle: Finding microscopic residue that prevents the perfect clean slate.

### subject_rotations

- Treating 'তেল' (oil) as a vector for linguistic decay—how flavor vocabulary changes when names change from *সিঙ্গাড়া* to *সামোসা*.
- Examining the oil through the lens of material science: how different molecular structures physically alter the dough structure, leading to textural dissonance.
- A purely internal monologue focusing on the speaker's saliva reacting differently—a physical manifestation of emotional duality (sweet vs. bitter memory).

### sensory_details

- Visual: The way mustard oil catches light with a deep, turbulent amber glaze, compared to the pale, static sheen of refined vegetable fat.
- Auditory: The specific high-frequency *thrum* produced when dense, pungent oils hit moderate heat versus the dull, absorbing *shush* of neutral fats.
- Tactile: The sticky, almost heavy film left on fingers after handling mustard oil vs. the slick, non-gripping coating from lighter industrial blends.

### research_questions

- What specific Bengali vocabulary exists that can describe an aroma as simultaneously 'sharp' and profoundly 'nostalgic'?
- Are there quantifiable differences in volatile organic compounds released by mustard oil vs. common neutral oils when heated to the same temperature?

### structural_variants

- A form structured around a mantra of repetition, where each iteration uses one new word from the lexicon (তেল, গন্ধ, মাটির)
- A shift in perspective between stanzas: 1-4 being external observation of the oil; 5-8 being internal reception of that aroma.

### unused_angles

- The politics of import substitution framed through kitchen staples.
- How memory fades not due to time, but due to successful environmental replacement (i.e., the foreign substitute is *better* at being neutral).

### variety_note

This chapter focuses exclusively on the primary chemical and sensory marker—the oil itself—diverging from previous chapters which focused on broader geographical or social shifts in food consumption.
