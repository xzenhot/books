## creative_enrichment

### angles

- Treating the oil not as a medium, but as a viscous map that distorts geographical coordinates.
- Framing the difference in smell (mustard sharp vs. neutral bland) through the lens of language failure—one is an active word, the other merely filler.
- A direct address to the cooking implement itself (e.g., 'হে তাওয়া' or 'ও কড়াই'), asking it if its memory persists despite the new substance.

### story_options

- Setting: A small, forgotten corner of a local Bengali grocery store; Desire: To find one packet of mustard oil whose label still shows the village name (old Bengal); Obstacle: All stock has been replaced by generic brand names.
- Setting: The dark interior of an old travel bag stuffed with cooking tools; Desire: To hear the distinct sound of *sorsher tel* hitting hot metal from memory; Obstacle: The bag only smells of stale, neutral airplane air.
- A scene where 'I' intentionally over-boil a tiny amount of oil to achieve a momentary chemical flare—a false sensory peak that immediately subsides.

### subject_rotations

- Focusing exclusively on the tactile experience: how the viscosity and weight of the different oils feel in the hand while pouring.
- Shifting the focus from 'taste' to 'preservation': viewing the oil as a preservative medium that fails when crossing borders, thus spoiling memory itself.
- Treating the act of smelling the heated oil as a momentary ritualistic performance rather than an organic experience.

### sensory_details

- The visual shimmer of pale yellow fat catching the harsh overhead light in the diaspora setting.
- A specific, acrid metallic tang that overlays any attempt to smell the authentic spice bloom.
- The slick, almost unnatural smoothness of the neutral oil when coating a spoon.

### research_questions

- What is the archaic Bengali vocabulary term for 'pungent' or 'sharpness' related specifically to cooking oil that carries more cultural weight than general descriptive words?
- How can I poetically represent the transition from *ghran* (scent) to *swad* (taste) when the chemical signal has been fundamentally altered?

### structural_variants

- A highly segmented, almost staccato structure in Segments 1 and 2 to mimic rapid sensory intake vs. slow, dull endurance.
- Using a central refrain—a single word like 'মাটি' or 'আঁচ'—that is repeated but modified semantically across all eight segments.

### unused_angles

- The specific labor involved in crushing seeds versus buying pre-pressed oil, focusing on the lost human effort.
- A contemplation of how the modern container (plastic/glass) fails to transmit the ancient properties of the vessel used for cooking.

### variety_note

This chapter focuses intensely inward on a single sensory input—the medium itself (oil)—a precise isolation contrasting with neighbour chapters that address broader market scenes or linguistic failures.
