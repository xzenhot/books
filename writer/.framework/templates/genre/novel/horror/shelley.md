# Book Analysis: Frankenstein

## Document Overview
**Source:** shelley.txt
**Work:** Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus (1818)
**Author:** Mary Shelley
**Genre:** Horror
**Structure:** Framed tale of creation, transgression, and pursuit

---

## Quality Metrics Analysis

### 1. The Architecture of Dread
**Rating: 9.5/10**

Horror builds unease through atmosphere, isolation, and the slow revelation of the thing that should not be. Dread is paced, not merely shown.

### 2. The Monstrous and the Human
**Rating: 9.5/10**

The genre's deep subject is the boundary between human and monster — often the monster is the more sympathetic, and the horror is in the mirror.

### 3. Transgression and Consequence
**Rating: 9/10**

Horror is moral in structure: something forbidden is crossed, and the crossing unlooses a consequence that cannot be called back.

### 4. The Uncanny
**Rating: 9/10**

The familiar made strange — the home, the body, the face — is the genre's native instrument of fear.

### 5. Cultural Resonance
**Rating: 9/10**

It gives shape to our deepest anxieties: death, isolation, the fear of what we ourselves have made.

## Metaphorical Landscape

### Primary Conceptual Framework
- **The Creation:** What we make may turn upon us
- **The Abandoned:** Isolation, rejection, and the monster's grief
- **The Pursuit:** Guilt and consequence that will not stop
- **The Body:** The uncanny — flesh, face, and the fear of the self
