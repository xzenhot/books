I never touched the letter. I want that clear from the start.

I was in the other room the whole time, and I can prove it — I was reading, and the clock said ten past, and when the letter was found on the table I was as surprised as anyone. More, probably. I had no reason to open it. What was in it was none of my business, and I have always respected the line between what is mine and what is not.

Of course I know what it said now. Everyone does. But that is only because she read it aloud, later, after everything. By then it was already out, and nothing I could have done would have put it back.

I don't remember whether the seal was broken when she found it. I've been asked that, and I've said I don't remember, because I don't. It's an honest answer. If I hesitate on that point, it is only because I am trying to be precise, and precision matters to me.

The truth is simple. I never touched the letter. And if my name was the one she kept repeating afterward — well, that only proves how little she understood me, and how much she wanted someone to blame.

I was reading. The clock said ten past. I never touched the letter.
