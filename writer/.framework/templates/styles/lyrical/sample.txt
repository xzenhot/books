The light came through the leaves the way a question comes through silence — slowly, and all at once.

She stood at the edge of the field, and the wind moved the wheat the way the sea moves a rumor. There was no grief here, only the long gold of evening leaning on the horizon, and the sound of a train far off, carrying its passengers toward a place where someone was waiting.

Memory is a window left open in the rain. It lets the weather in. It lets the weather remind you what you were trying to forget.

She closed her eyes. The day turned. The wheat bowed once, as if it had understood something it would never say. And the light, patient as water, went on filling the field with its slow, unbroken music.
