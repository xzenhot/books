I did not mean to stay so long at the window. But the light was leaving, and I have always watched the light leave as if it were taking something of mine with it.

I remember the day they went. I remember it wrong, I am almost sure of that now — I remember the coat thrown over the chair, and the way the door clicked, though they said afterwards there was no click, the latch had been broken for weeks. I have invented the click. I hear it still, clean as a coin dropped.

What I cannot remember is the last thing I said. That is the part I go back to, turning it over like a stone, hoping the underside will be written. It is blank. That is what I know of myself: I watched the light leave, and I let the last words go unsaid, and now I hear a click that never happened, clean as a coin.

The room is cold. I tell myself that is the weather. But it is not the weather.
