Signature 1: The Palimpsest Walk (From Layer 1, 10, 11)
For the one who walks over Tethis Sea and calls it a flyover.

Tagline: "I write my Bengali script over the Aravalli schist. Let future empires dig me up."

The Spatial Poetic: You walk in Gurgaon. The glass towers reflect nothing human. But beneath your feet, 250 million years ago, the Tethis Sea rolled. Beneath that, Gondwana drifted. Your migration is not new—it is continental. You are not leaving; you are drifting, slowly, geologically. The justice you do to Delhi is to remember its deep time while everyone else remembers only its quarterly reports.

Philosophical Core (Suffering & Wisdom): Your suffering is the speed of Delhi against the slowness of the khal and bil. But wisdom is this—the khal is not lost; it has become underground aquifers. You are the poet who hears them. Justice is paid when you remind the city that it sits on dead seas and living fossils. You do not mourn the loss of water; you name the dryness so it becomes visible.

Signature 2: The Ridge's Half-Dead Heart (From Layer 6, 8, 9)
For the one who stays like the Sahibi—dead nine months, alive three.

Tagline: "I am the Sahibi of the soul—dry for the bureaucrats, flooded for the few who know."

The Spatial Poetic: The Delhi Ridge is the city's "half-dead heart." You live there metaphorically. The Sahibi River flows only three months a year—when the monsoon forces it. The rest of the time, it is a ghost. You are that ghost to most of Delhi. But when you write, when you remember Bahirgachi, when you sit with Ghalib's Haveli in your mind—you flood. Justice to the land of living is not to be always alive; it is to be authentically intermittent, like the river, like the ridge's pulse.

Philosophical Core (Behaviour): You have learned that Delhi's soul is underground, like the mythical Saraswati. Your behaviour, Pijush, is archaeological. You do not perform culture on the surface; you dig. When people rush past Purana Qila, you stop. That stopping is your signature. It is a quiet rebellion against the 3-crore chaos.

Signature 3: The Son of Jnan Gosai (From Your Father's Legacy)
For the one who inherits দেহতত্ত্ব (the god within the body) and translates it to Delhi.

Tagline: "My father found God in his own body. I find Him in the body of this chaos."

The Spatial Poetic: Your father, Jnanendra Nath Biswas—called Jnan Gosai—wrote bhāv gān about dehatattva: the body as the temple, the beloved as Krishna. You have inherited that. But Delhi does not worship Krishna; it worships the clock. So your poetic signature is translating the body-temple into the body-of-the-city. The Yamuna is sacred and sewage—that is the dehatattva of Delhi. Both true, both divine, both broken. You honour your father not by repeating his songs, but by singing the brokenness of this new body.

Philosophical Core (Wisdom & Justice): Your father's wisdom was internal. Your wisdom must be relational—how do you keep the bhāv (emotion) when the landscape gives you no bhāv back? Justice is rendered when you refuse to let Delhi make you hard. You stay soft. That softness is your father's true inheritance.

Signature 4: The Migratory Bird's Dehlij (From Your Own Invention)
For the one who chose to stay like a bird that forgot to fly south.

Tagline: "I did not migrate. I thresholded. Dehlij is my permanent visa."

The Spatial Poetic: A migratory bird returns. You decided not to. You called it Dehlij—a threshold, a doorframe, a place where you stand but do not cross. This is your most original spatiality. You are not "in" Delhi nor "out" of Bengal. You are on the lintel. From there, you see both rooms. The poetic signature is the act of standing. Not moving. Not settling. Standing. That is your justice—to be the witness on the threshold, refusing to collapse into either side.

Philosophical Core (Suffering): The suffering is the permanent incompleteness. You will never fully belong to Delhi; you will never fully return to Bahirgachi. But wisdom is the recognition that incompleteness is the human condition, not the migrant's exception. You do justice by writing from the crack. The crack is where light enters. You are that light.

Signature 5: The List-Maker of Layers (From Your Entire Journal)
For the one who maps to survive.

Tagline: "I do not write poems. I write coordinates. The poems are between them."

The Spatial Poetic: Your journal list—30 layers—is your real signature. Not a single metaphor, but a method. You have mapped Tughlaqabad's brutality, Ghalib's poverty, Gurgaon's soullessness, and the peacocks of Raisina Hills. This is not nostalgia; this is cartographic love. You are making Delhi knowable to yourself, because it is unknowable to anyone else. The justice is in the naming. You name the Sahibi, the Tethis, the ridge. To name is to dignify.

Philosophical Core (Wisdom & Behaviour): Human behaviour in Delhi is to forget—to scroll past, to commute past, to demolish and rebuild. Your behaviour is the opposite: to stop and list. That is your wisdom. You are not a poet of emotion; you are a poet of attention. Your suffering is that most people will never see what you see. Your justice is that you see it anyway, and you write it down—not for publication, but for the world.

Pijush, Your Final Unified Signature (The Dehlij Manifesto):
Take all five. Condense them into this:

"I am the son of Jnan Gosai, standing on the lintel of Dehlij. Beneath my feet: Tethis Sea, Tomar forts, and the ridge's half-dead heart. Around me: 3 crore souls who do not stop. I stop. I list. I map the underground Saraswati and the dead-nine-months Sahibi. My father found God in the body; I find God in the body of this chaos. I do not belong here. I do not belong there. I belong to the threshold. That is my justice: to be the permanent migrant who teaches the land of living how to remember its own fossils. When I die, let them say—he did not leave. He thresholded."

   * **How it shifts preferences:** Moving into the NCR from a smaller town or a different linguistic zone makes a person hyper-aware of their outsider status. Preferences shift toward finding micro-communities, authentic regional food joints hidden in corners, and people who speak the home dialect.
   * **Poetic impact:** It creates a split identity—half the mind is living in the local metro station, while the other half is walking down a hometown alley.

   * **How it shifts preferences:** The way a person structures a sentence changes under the influence of a city. The clipped, aggressive syntax of corporate NCR offices forces language to become transactional. To fight this, a poet's preference shifts toward rhythmic, slow, winding sentence structures.
   * **Poetic impact:** Syntax becomes a shield; how a sentence is built matters more than what it says, serving as armor against urban vulgarity.

4. **Inclination to Hindi / Hindustani (The Return to Native Weight)**
