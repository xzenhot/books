At the same hour that the old man rose from his chair to face the door, the woman across the river was setting down her lamp, and neither knew that the other moved, though both were turning toward the same remembered night, the same wrong done thirty years ago that neither had ever spoken of.

The old man did not know that the woman still kept his name folded in a book. The woman did not know that the old man still rose at that hour every evening, as if the door owed him an arrival. Each believed the other had forgotten. Each was wrong, and would remain wrong, because the river between them was not water but the silence they had both chosen to keep.

Above them the stars held their ancient places, indifferent to the two small lives that had once touched and now only turned in their separate rooms, at the same hour, toward the same door. The pattern was already complete. It had only to be waited out, as the old man waited, as the woman set down her lamp, as the river went on being a silence neither would cross.
