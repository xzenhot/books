The era from 1960 to 2020 marks a radical shift in text-based poetry. Poets walked away from the heavy, complex academic style of early modernism. Instead, they embraced raw emotion, identity politics, structural games, performance, and eventually, the fast-paced nature of the internet.
Here is how text-based poetry evolved across these five major movements:

1. Confessionalism (1960s–1970s)

• The Philosophy: Poetic expression as a raw, psychological unmasking of the private self.
• The Text Style: First-person ("I") narratives that shattered social taboos. For the first time, poets wrote explicitly about mental illness, trauma, suicide, difficult marriages, and family dysfunction. It traded polite metaphors for shocking, brutal honesty.
• Key Figures: Sylvia Plath, Robert Lowell, Anne Sexton.

2. Language Poetry (1970s–1980s)

• The Philosophy: The belief that language creates meaning, rather than just reporting it.
• The Text Style: Highly abstract and non-linear. These poets deliberately broke grammar, disrupted sentence structure, and threw out traditional storytelling. The reader is forced to piece together meaning from fragmented, floating words, turning reading into an active puzzle.
• Key Figures: Charles Bernstein, Lyn Hejinian, Ron Silliman.

3. New Formalism (1980s–1990s)

• The Philosophy: A rebellious return to traditional boundaries after decades of free verse dominance.
• The Text Style: A strict re-adoption of classical meter (like iambic pentameter), rhyme schemes, and fixed forms (like sonnets and villanelles). However, they applied these ancient rules to contemporary, everyday stories and colloquial, modern speech.
• Key Figures: Dana Gioia, Marilyn Hacker, Vikram Seth.

4. Spoken Word & Slam Poetry (1990s–2000s)

• The Philosophy: Returning poetry to its ancient, oral roots as a tool for community storytelling and political protest.
• The Text Style: Rhythmic, urgent, and heavily reliant on musicality, cadence, and breath. The text is written explicitly to be performed aloud. It frequently addresses systemic racism, intersectional feminism, queer identity, and economic inequality.
• Key Figures: Marc Smith (founder of poetry slam), Patricia Smith, Saul Williams.

5. Instapoetry (2010s–2020)

• The Philosophy: Democratizing poetry by making it universally accessible and instantly shareable via digital screens.
• The Text Style: Ultra-minimalist, using short lines, lowercase text, and zero complex metaphors. It focuses on universal themes like healing, heartbreak, and self-love. The poems are specifically styled visually to fit into a smartphone screen, often paired with simple line drawings.
• Key Figures: Rupi Kaur, Warsan Shire, Yrsa Daley-Ward.
