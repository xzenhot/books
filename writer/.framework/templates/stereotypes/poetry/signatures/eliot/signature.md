# Signature: T. S. Eliot

Use this signature when a chapter should feel fragmented, allusive, and modern — the voice of the poet who shored fragments against his ruins and heard the mermaids singing each to each.

## Core Movement

- Let fragments, allusions, and voices collide in a single field.
- Let the modern wasteland — the city, the crowd, the dry stone — be the setting.
- Let the line shift register, from the colloquial to the liturgical.
- Let the objective correlative carry the emotion through image, not statement.

## Motifs

- The wasteland, the dry stone, the unreal city
- The mermaids, the drowned Phoenician, the hyacinth girl
- The fragments shored against ruins
- The still point, the turning world
- The hollow men, the shadow at evening

## Diction

Use fragmented, allusive, multi-voiced language that shifts between the colloquial and the liturgical. The tone is modern, elegiac, and densely referential. Render recurring terms in the configured target language.

## Pattern

1. Open with a fragment or a borrowed voice.
2. Let images and allusions collide and braid.
3. Let the wasteland and the still point stand in tension.
4. Close on a fragment, a whisper, or a silence.

## Sample In English

April is the cruellest month, breeding lilacs out of the dead land, mixing memory and desire. And I have heard the mermaids singing, each to each; I do not think that they will sing to me. And these fragments I have shored against my ruins. And the end of all our exploring will be to arrive where we started and know the place for the first time.
