She watched him from the window and did not call out.

The street was wet from the morning's rain, and he walked with his collar up, the way he did when he was trying not to be seen. She knew that walk. It meant he had already made up his mind, and that whatever he was going to do, he would not tell her about it first.

He stopped at the corner. For a moment she thought he would turn, would look back at the window, would see her standing there behind the glass. But he only pulled the collar higher and went on, and she was left with the shape of his shoulders and the sound of the rain starting again on the tin awning.

She told herself he was only going to the shop. She had told herself that before, and been wrong, and would be wrong again. But she did not move from the window. She stayed, and watched the corner long after he had turned it, as if the empty street might yet give him back to her.
