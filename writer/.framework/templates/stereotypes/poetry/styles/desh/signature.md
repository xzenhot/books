# Desh Style — Poetry Specification

A craft specification for the **Desh** (দেশ) poetic voice, rebuilt from and grounded in the ten poems of `.framework/templates/stereotypes/poetry/styles/desh/examples.md`. That file is the canonical example corpus: every rule below is derived from what those ten poems actually do, and it should be read first and consulted alongside this spec.

The voice is **dense, esoteric, and tantric-mythological** — a first-person consciousness that dissects its own body and its own era through corpse-ritual, serpent, blood, and cosmic imagery. It is not a polished editorial lyric; it is an incantatory, transgressive monologue spoken from the cremation ground of the self.

## 2. Voice & Identity

- **First-person, confessional, self-accusing.** The speaker indicts itself: *"এই তো আমি। দণ্ডপ্রাপক।"* (৪), *"নিজের মড়া উল্টো ক'রে শবসাধনার হোতা।"* (৪). The "I" is both priest and corpse.
- **Incantatory and prophetic.** Long, winding, breath-heavy utterances that build toward a litany or a self-negation.
- **Addresses a "you"** — sometimes the reader (৭: *"পাঠক! পাঠক"*), sometimes the self, sometimes an absent beloved — without ever resolving who is listening.

---

## 3. Register & Vocabulary

- **Heavy *tatsama* (Sanskritic) diction.** The poems lean into the oldest, most esoteric layer of Bengali rather than the polished everyday: বীভৎস, শৃগালতাড়িত, অনঙ্গ, ভবগর্ভ, জরা, তামসিক, সত্ত্বগুণী, ক্ষণচিহ্ণমালাপরিহিতা, শ্মশানচারী, জগদ্ধারিণী, ত্রিকাল, দণ্ডপ্রাপক, প্রপঞ্চ, প্রমাদ, চিদাকাশ, সহস্রার.
- **A tantric/mythological lexicon.** কালী, শবসাধনা, শৃগাল, শ্মশান, কবন্ধ, শকুন, বোধি, তথাগত, ধধীচি, মনসারর্ম, ইষ্টনাম, ইষ্টরতি, যোগিনী, নীহারিকা.
- **Deliberately transgressive diction fused with the high register.** Crude, bodily words sit inside elevated syntax: *"ওয়াক্ গর্ভের বমি"* (৫), *"লীলাশিশ্ন"* (১), *"জারজ"* (৫), *"বিচি"* (৫). The collision of the sacred and the obscene is the point, not an accident.

## 4. Imagery & Motif Families

Every image in the corpus belongs to one of a few connected families. A new poem should draw from these rather than invent unrelated decoration.

### 4.1 Corpse & Cremation
মড়া, শবসাধনা, শ্মশান, শৃগাল (jackal), শকুন (vulture), কবন্ধ (headless torso), করোটি (skull), হাড়, লোলচর্ম. The body is always already becoming a corpse; meditation on the corpse is the spiritual method (শবসাধনা).

### 4.2 Blood & Violence
রক্তপথ, রক্তফিনকি, বিষদাঁত, কোপ, ছোবল, ফনা. Violence is intimate and close, a bite or a blade rather than a battlefield.

### 4.3 The Serpent
সাপ, সহস্রার, ফনা, বিষ, ছোবল. The snake is at once the reader, the critic, the poison, and the awakened kundalini (সহস্রার).

### 4.4 Cosmic & Mythic
কালী, আকাশ, নীহারিকা, ব্রহ্ম, চিদাকাশ, গ্রহ, ত্রিকাল. The cosmic and the bodily collapse into one another — the sky wears কালীর costume (১).

### 4.5 The Dissolving Body
পেট, শ্বাপদ (the beast in the belly), দেহ, লোলচর্ম, মাংস, স্নায়ু. The body is a site where the self is eaten, vomited, or born again — not a stable container.

### 4.6 Reading & Poetry (the meta-poetic strain)
পাঠ, গ্রন্থ, বোধিগাছ, বাংলা কবিতা, পাঠক, শ্লোক. Poetry repeatedly turns on itself: the act of reading is figured as regurgitation (৯), seduction, or a snake's bite (৭).

## 5. Themes

1. **Death & decay** — not as an event but as a continuous state.
2. **Self-dissolution & self-loathing** — the speaker is its own judge and executioner.
3. **Transgression** — sex, prostitution, bastardy, and the obscene are named plainly, without euphemism or apology.
4. **Urban violence** — the crowd, the bridge, the hawker's severed head (৩), the neon advertisement as a fang (৭).
5. **Existential loneliness** — a "কেউ প্রশ্নাতীত ভালো আছে" that only sharpens the speaker's own solitude (১০).
6. **Poetry itself** — the condition of the reader, the critic, and the poem in the Bengali marketplace is a recurring subject, often treated with contempt and love at once.

---

## 6. Form & Architecture

- **Two forms coexist.** Some poems are unbroken prose blocks (১, ২, ৪, ৬); others break into short lines (৩, ৫, ৭, ৮, ৯, ১০). Both are valid; choose by the pressure of the material.
- **Long, enjambed, winding sentences.** Clauses pile up and turn on themselves; breath is the unit, not the line.
- **Anaphora & litany.** Poem ৩ builds entirely on *"এসব… এসব… এসব…"*; poem ৪ on *"এক ভ্রমণে…"*. Repetition is a structural engine.
- **The rhetorical question as refrain.** *"কার ভিতরের পাপ খসে না?"* (৪), *"কী করব? কীভাবে…?"* (৮), *"এ-ই কি উৎস্রোত? এ-ই পাঠাভ্যাস?"* (৯). Questions do not seek answers; they widen the wound.
- **Circular return.** Poem ১০ opens *"কেউ প্রশ্নাতীত ভালো আছে জেনে একা লাগে আজ"* and closes *"কেউ, দূরে, স্থির ভালো আছে। একা লাগে, প্রশ্নাতীত একা লাগে আজ।"* The end folds back onto the beginning without resolving it.

## 8. Writing Checklist

| Element | Target |
|---|---|
| **Register** | Dense, esoteric, *tatsama*-heavy Bengali with a tantric/mythological lexicon |
| **Voice** | First-person, confessional, self-accusing, incantatory |
| **Imagery** | Draw from the six motif families (corpse, blood, serpent, cosmic, dissolving body, reading/poetry) |
| **Transgression** | Name the obscene and the sacred in the same breath, without euphemism |
| **Form** | Prose block or line-broken; long enjambed sentences; anaphora allowed |
| **Closure** | Unresolved — a rhetorical question, a paradox, or a circular return, never a tidy resolution |
| **Meta-poetic strain** | Let poetry/the reader/the critic surface as subject where the material demands it |

---

## 9. Using the Examples

When writing in this style, open `examples.md` and read it in full. Treat the ten poems as the model for tone, diction, and image-choice. Do not imitate their lines; imitate their method — the way they collapse the cosmic into the bodily, name the obscene plainly, and end without comfort. Where this spec is ambiguous, the examples are the final authority.
