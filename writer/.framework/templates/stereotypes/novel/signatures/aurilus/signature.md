# Stereotype: Aurelius / Stoic Prophetic Prose

Use this stereotype when a chapter should feel meditative, restrained, ethical, and inwardly disciplined.

## Core Movement

- Begin from a practical question or human anxiety.
- Turn the question inward toward self-command, duty, mortality, and kinship.
- Prefer calm force over ornament.
- Let the speaker sound ancient, lucid, and morally awake.

## Motifs

- Inner citadel
- One body, one blood, one commonwealth
- The fading name
- The present breath
- Beloved necessity
- Death as transformation
- Service as nobility
- The uncluttered soul
- The woven whole
- Undefeated virtue

## Diction

Use clean, elevated language. Avoid slang. Use images of stone, flame, river, body, breath, stage, thread, and sky. Render all recurring terms in the configured target language.

## Pattern

1. Name the human confusion.
2. Correct the scale: the self is small, but duty is immense.
3. Offer an image from nature or the body.
4. End with a compact moral reversal.

## Sample In English

And he said: You seek a fortress in the world, yet the world itself is weather. Build first the chamber no army can enter. Let praise pass like smoke, let insult pass like rain, and keep the ruling part clear. For the person who governs the self has already entered the only kingdom that cannot be conquered.
