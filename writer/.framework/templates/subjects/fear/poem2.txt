# Sample 2 — Fear

## Gitanjali 40

**By Rabindranath Tagore** (Bengal, India, 1861–1941)

*Source: Gitanjali (Song Offerings), translated into English by the author, 1912. Public domain worldwide.*

---

The rain has held back for days and days, my God, in my arid heart. The horizon
is fiercely naked—not the thinnest cover of a soft cloud, not the vaguest hint of
a distant cool shower.

Send thy angry storm, dark with death, if it is thy wish, and with lashes of
lightning startle the sky from end to end.

But call back, my lord, call back this pervading silent heat, still and keen
and cruel, burning the heart with dire despair.

Let the cloud of grace bend low from above like the tearful look of the mother
on the day of the father's wrath.

---

## Notes

- **Poet:** Rabindranath Tagore, the Bengali poet and Nobel laureate (1913).
- **Country:** Bengal, India.
- **Era:** Early 20th century; Gitanjali was published in English in 1912.
- **Theme:** Fear and its relief — the arid heart that longs even for a storm, if only the silent, burning despair would end, and the prayer for the cloud of grace to bend low.
- **Contrast:** Where Poe's "The Raven" (sample1) dwells in fear and dread, Tagore's poem turns fear into a prayer. Together they show fear as both the terror and the longing for deliverance.
