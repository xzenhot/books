# Prose Sample 1 — Childhood

## David Copperfield (Chapter I)

**By Charles Dickens** (England, 1812–1870)

*Source: David Copperfield (1850). Public domain worldwide.*

---

Whether I shall turn out to be the hero of my own life, or whether that station
will be held by anybody else, these pages must show. To begin my life with the
beginning of my life, I record that I was born (as I have been informed and
believe) on a Friday, at twelve o'clock at night. It was remarked that the clock
began to strike, and I began to cry, simultaneously.

In consideration of the day and hour of my birth, it was declared by the
nurse, and by some sage women in the neighbourhood who had taken a lively interest
in me several months before there was any possibility of our becoming personally
acquainted, first, that I was destined to be unlucky in life; and secondly, that
I was privileged to see ghosts and spirits; both these gifts inevitably
attaching, as they believed, to all unlucky infants of either gender, born towards the
small hours on a Friday night.

I need say nothing here, on the first head, because nothing can show better
than my history whether that prediction was verified or falsified by the result.
On the second branch of the question, I will only remark, that unless I ran
through that part of my inheritance while I was a baby, I have not come into it yet.

---

## Notes

- **Writer:** Charles Dickens, the English novelist.
- **Country:** England.
- **Era:** Mid-19th century; the novel was published in 1850.
- **Theme:** The beginning of a life — the child's birth told in the first person, with the superstitions and omens that surround it, and the question of whether one is the hero of one's own life.
- **Contrast:** Where the poems in this subject see childhood from a distance, as innocence or play, Dickens enters childhood from within, in the voice of the child grown up and looking back.
