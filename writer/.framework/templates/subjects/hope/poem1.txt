# Sample 1 — Hope

## "Hope" is the thing with feathers

**By Emily Dickinson** (United States, 1830–1886)

*Source: Poems (1891). Public domain worldwide.*

---

"Hope" is the thing with feathers –
That perches in the soul –
And sings the tune without the words –
And never stops – at all –

And sweetest – in the Gale – is heard –
And sore must be the storm –
That could abash the little Bird
That kept so many warm –

I've heard it in the chillest land –
And on the strangest Sea –
Yet – never – in Extremity,
It asked a crumb – of me.

---

## Notes

- **Poet:** Emily Dickinson, the American poet of Amherst, Massachusetts.
- **Country:** United States.
- **Era:** Mid-19th century; the poem was published posthumously in 1891.
- **Theme:** Hope as a bird in the soul — a thing with feathers that sings without words, never stops, and asks nothing in return, even in the chillest land.
- **Contrast:** This is hope as a quiet, persistent presence. It pairs with Tagore's Gitanjali 37 (sample2), which sees hope as a journey — the thought that travels on, singing, through the wilderness.
