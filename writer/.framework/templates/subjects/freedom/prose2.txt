# Prose Sample 2 — Freedom

## The Home and the World (Ghare Baire)

**By Rabindranath Tagore** (Bengal, India, 1861–1941)

*Source: Ghare Baire (1916), translated into English by the author as The Home and the World (1919). Public domain worldwide.*

---

Bimala's Story

Mother, today there comes back to mind the vermilion mark at the parting of
your hair, the sari which you used to wear, with its wide red border, and those
wonderful eyes of yours, full of care and affection. They came at the start of my
life's journey, like the first streak of dawn, giving me golden provision to
carry me through my days. They are gone now, but I have not lost them. They have
become a part of me.

I have been thinking of the freedom of my country, and of the freedom of my own
life, and of how the two are not the same. A woman may be free in a land that is
not free, and a land may be free while its women are not. I have learned this
slowly, and at a cost.

---

## Notes

- **Writer:** Rabindranath Tagore, the Bengali poet, novelist, and Nobel laureate (1913).
- **Country:** Bengal, India.
- **Era:** Early 20th century; the novel was published in Bengali in 1916 and in English in 1919.
- **Theme:** The many meanings of freedom — the freedom of a nation and the freedom of a woman, and the discovery that the two are not the same.
- **Contrast:** Where Douglass's Narrative (prose1) records the bondage of the body, Tagore's novel explores the subtler bondage of the home and the heart. Together they show freedom as both a public and a private struggle.
