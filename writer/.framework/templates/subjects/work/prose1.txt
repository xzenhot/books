# Prose Sample 1 — Work

## The Jungle

**By Upton Sinclair** (United States, 1878–1968)

*Source: The Jungle (1906). Public domain worldwide.*

---

It was four o'clock when the ceremony was over and the carriages began to
arrive. There had been a crowd following all the way, owing to the exuberance of
Marija Berczynskas. The occasion rested heavily upon Marija's broad shoulders—it
was her task to see that all things went in due form, and after the best home
traditions; and, flying wildly hither and thither, bowling every one out of the
way, and scolding and exhorting all day long, she came upon the scene of the
wedding feast, and, with a great flourish, seated herself at the head of the table.

The room was about thirty feet square, with whitewashed walls, bare save for a
calendar, a picture of a race horse, and a family tree in a gilded frame. To the
right there was a door from the saloon, with a few loafers in the doorway, and in
the corner beyond it a bar, with a presiding genius clad in soiled white, with
waxed black mustaches and a carefully oiled curl plastered against one side of
his forehead. In the opposite corner were two tables, filling a third of the room
and laden with dishes and cold viands, which a few of the hungrier guests were
already munching.

---

## Notes

- **Writer:** Upton Sinclair, the American novelist and muckraking journalist.
- **Country:** United States.
- **Era:** Early 20th century; the novel was published in 1906.
- **Theme:** Work and its cost — the novel that exposed the brutal conditions of the Chicago meatpacking industry, where immigrant labor was ground down by the machinery of industry.
- **Contrast:** Where the poems in this subject honor work as pride and worship, Sinclair's prose shows work as exploitation — the labor that consumes the laborer. Together they show work's two faces — the dignity and the drudgery.
