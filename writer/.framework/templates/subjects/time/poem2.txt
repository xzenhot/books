# Sample 2 — Time

## Gitanjali 82

**By Rabindranath Tagore** (Bengal, India, 1861–1941)

*Source: Gitanjali (Song Offerings), translated into English by the author, 1912. Public domain worldwide.*

---

Time is endless in thy hands, my lord. There is none to count thy minutes.

Days and nights pass and ages bloom and fade like flowers. Thou knowest how to
wait.

Thy centuries follow each other perfecting a small wild flower.

We have no time to lose, and having no time we must scramble for our chances.
We are too poor to be late.

And thus it is that time goes by while I give it to every querulous man who
claims it, and thine altar is empty of all offerings to the last.

At the end of the day I hasten in fear lest thy gate be shut; but I find that
yet there is time.

---

## Notes

- **Poet:** Rabindranath Tagore, the Bengali poet and Nobel laureate (1913).
- **Country:** Bengal, India.
- **Era:** Early 20th century; Gitanjali was published in English in 1912.
- **Theme:** Time as endless in the hands of the divine — the contrast between our frantic haste and God's patient waiting, and the reassurance that "yet there is time."
- **Contrast:** Where Marvell's poem (sample1) hears time hurrying and urges haste, Tagore's poem rests in the knowledge that time is endless. Together they show time as both the chariot and the patient hand.
