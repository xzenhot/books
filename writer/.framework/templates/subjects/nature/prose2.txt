# Prose Sample 2 — Nature

## Aranyak (Of the Forest)

**By Bibhutibhushan Bandyopadhyay** (Bengal, India, 1894–1950)

*Source: Aranyak (1939), a Bengali novel. Public domain in India; translated into many languages.*

---

The forest began where the fields ended. At first it was only a few scattered
trees, and then, all at once, it closed around me — a green darkness, cool and
full of sound. The sun came down through the leaves in broken pieces, and the
ground was soft with the fallen leaves of many years.

I had come to this forest to be alone, and yet I was not alone. There was a
life here that had nothing to do with me — the life of the trees, the birds, the
small animals that moved unseen through the undergrowth. It went on around me,
indifferent and complete, and in its presence I felt my own smallness, and my own
peace.

I thought of the city I had left, and of the people there, and of all the
things that had seemed so important. Here, in the forest, they seemed very far
away, and very small.

---

## Notes

- **Writer:** Bibhutibhushan Bandyopadhyay, the Bengali novelist of rural Bengal.
- **Country:** Bengal, India.
- **Era:** Mid-20th century; the novel was published in 1939.
- **Theme:** The forest as refuge and teacher — a place where the self is quieted and the world's concerns fall away, in the presence of a life that is indifferent and complete.
- **Contrast:** Where Thoreau's Walden (prose1) reads nature as a mirror of the self, Bibhutibhushan's forest is a world apart from the self — a place to lose, not find, one's own importance.
