# Prose Sample 2 — Love

## Devdas

**By Sarat Chandra Chattopadhyay** (Bengal, India, 1876–1938)

*Source: Devdas (1917), a Bengali novel. Public domain in India; translated into many languages.*

---

Devdas was returning home after many years. The village of his childhood lay
before him, unchanged, and yet everything in it seemed smaller than he
remembered. The road, the trees, the pond — all had shrunk in his absence, as if the
world of his boyhood had been folded away and put aside.

He had not written to Parvati. He had not dared. And yet it was for her that he
had come, though he would not have said so, even to himself.

At the edge of the village he stopped. A girl was drawing water at the well,
and when she turned he saw that it was Parvati — Paro, as he had called her when
they were children. She had grown, and in her face there was something that had
not been there before: a quietness, a waiting, that made his heart turn over.

"Devdas," she said, and the name was enough.

---

## Notes

- **Writer:** Sarat Chandra Chattopadhyay, one of the most beloved Bengali novelists of the early twentieth century.
- **Country:** Bengal, India.
- **Era:** Early 20th century; Devdas was published in 1917.
- **Theme:** Love thwarted by caste, family, and pride — the tragedy of a love that is real but cannot be fulfilled, and the self-destruction that follows its loss.
- **Contrast:** Where Austen's Pride and Prejudice (prose1) lets love overcome social obstacles through wit and patience, Devdas shows love crushed by those same obstacles, ending in ruin. Together they show love's two outcomes — the comedy and the tragedy.
