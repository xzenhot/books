# Sample 1 — Justice

## The Cry of the Children

**By Elizabeth Barrett Browning** (England, 1806–1861)

*Source: Poems (1844). Public domain worldwide.*

---

Do ye hear the children weeping, O my brothers,
    Ere the sorrow comes with years?
They are leaning their young heads against their mothers,
    And that cannot stop their tears.
The young lambs are bleating in the meadows,
    The young birds are chirping in the nest,
The young fawns are playing with the shadows,
    The young flowers are blowing toward the west—
But the young, young children, O my brothers,
    They are weeping bitterly!
They are weeping in the playtime of the others,
    In the country of the free.

---

## Notes

- **Poet:** Elizabeth Barrett Browning, the English poet of the Victorian era.
- **Country:** England.
- **Era:** Mid-19th century; the poem was published in 1844.
- **Theme:** The cry for justice — the children forced to labor in mines and factories, weeping "in the country of the free," and the poet's protest against their suffering.
- **Contrast:** This is justice as a cry against wrong. It pairs with Tagore's Gitanjali 35 (sample2), which imagines justice as a whole world — a "heaven of freedom" where the mind is without fear.
