# Sample 1 — Sacrifice

## The Charge of the Light Brigade

**By Alfred, Lord Tennyson** (England, 1809–1892)

*Source: Maud, and Other Poems (1855). Public domain worldwide.*

---

Half a league, half a league,
    Half a league onward,
All in the valley of Death
    Rode the six hundred.
"Forward, the Light Brigade!
Charge for the guns!" he said.
Into the valley of Death
    Rode the six hundred.

"Forward, the Light Brigade!"
Was there a man dismayed?
Not though the soldier knew
    Some one had blundered.
Theirs not to make reply,
Theirs not to reason why,
Theirs but to do and die.
Into the valley of Death
    Rode the six hundred.

---

## Notes

- **Poet:** Alfred, Lord Tennyson, the English Poet Laureate.
- **Country:** England.
- **Era:** Mid-19th century; the poem was published in 1855, after the Battle of Balaclava in the Crimean War.
- **Theme:** Sacrifice and duty — the six hundred who rode into the valley of Death, obeying an order they knew was a blunder, and the glory and the waste of their sacrifice.
- **Contrast:** This is sacrifice as martial duty. It pairs with Tagore's Gitanjali 90 (sample2), which sees sacrifice as a quiet, willing gift of the self to the divine.
