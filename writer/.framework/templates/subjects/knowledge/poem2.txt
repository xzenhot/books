# Sample 2 — Knowledge

## Gitanjali 12

**By Rabindranath Tagore** (Bengal, India, 1861–1941)

*Source: Gitanjali (Song Offerings), translated into English by the author, 1912. Public domain worldwide.*

---

The time that my journey takes is long and the way of it long.

I came out on the chariot of the first gleam of light, and pursued my voyage
through the wildernesses of worlds leaving my track on many a star and planet.

It is the most distant course that comes nearest to thyself, and that training
is the most intricate which leads to the utter simplicity of a tune.

The traveller has to knock at every alien door to come to his own, and one has
to wander through all the outer worlds to reach the innermost shrine at the end.

My eyes strayed far and wide before I shut them and said "Here art thou!"

The question and the cry "Oh, where?" melt into tears of a thousand streams and
deluge the world with the flood of the assurance "I am!"

---

## Notes

- **Poet:** Rabindranath Tagore, the Bengali poet and Nobel laureate (1913).
- **Country:** Bengal, India.
- **Era:** Early 20th century; Gitanjali was published in English in 1912.
- **Theme:** The long road of knowledge — the traveller who must wander through all the outer worlds to reach the innermost shrine, and find that the most distant course comes nearest to the self.
- **Contrast:** Where Tennyson's "Ulysses" (sample1) pursues knowledge outward, ever beyond, Tagore's poem turns knowledge inward, toward the self. Together they show knowledge as both the outward quest and the inward return.
