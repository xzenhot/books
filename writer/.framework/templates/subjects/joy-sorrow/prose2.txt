# Prose Sample 2 — Joy and Sorrow

## The Prophet (On Joy and Sorrow)

**By Kahlil Gibran** (Lebanon, 1883–1931)

*Source: The Prophet (1923). Public domain worldwide.*

---

Then a woman said, Speak to us of Joy and Sorrow.

And he answered:

Your joy is your sorrow unmasked.

And the selfsame well from which your laughter rises was oftentimes filled with
your tears.

And how else can it be?

The deeper that sorrow carves into your being, the more joy you can contain.

Is not the cup that holds your wine the very cup that was burned in the
potter's oven?

And is not the lute that soothes your spirit, the very wood that was hollowed
with knives?

When you are joyous, look deep into your heart and you shall find it is only
that which has given you sorrow that is giving you joy.

When you are sorrowful look again in your heart, and you shall see that in
truth you are weeping for that which has been your delight.

---

## Notes

- **Writer:** Kahlil Gibran, the Lebanese-American poet and philosopher.
- **Country:** Lebanon (writing in English, from the United States).
- **Era:** Early 20th century; The Prophet was published in 1923.
- **Theme:** Joy and sorrow as one — "your joy is your sorrow unmasked," and the deeper sorrow carves, the more joy the heart can contain.
- **Contrast:** Where Dickens's Tale of Two Cities (prose1) declares the union of joy and sorrow in a single age, Gibran's prose declares it in a single heart. Together they show the two faces of one heart, in history and in the self.
