# Sample 1 — Friendship

## A Time to Talk

**By Robert Frost** (United States, 1874–1963)

*Source: Mountain Interval (1916). Public domain worldwide.*

---

When a friend calls to me from the road
And slows his horse to a meaning walk,
I don't stand still and look around
On all the hills I haven't hoed,
And shout from where I am, What is it?
No, not as there is a time to talk.
I thrust my hoe in the mellow ground,
Blade-end up and five feet tall,
And plod: I go up to the stone wall
For a friendly visit.

---

## Notes

- **Poet:** Robert Frost, the American poet of rural New England.
- **Country:** United States.
- **Era:** Early 20th century; the poem was published in 1916.
- **Theme:** The priority of friendship — the farmer who stops his work and goes to the wall when a friend calls, because there is a time to talk.
- **Contrast:** This is friendship as a simple, unhurried presence. It pairs with Tagore's Gitanjali 63 (sample2), which sees friendship as a bond that time and distance cannot break.
