# Sample 2 — Sacrifice

## Gitanjali 90

**By Rabindranath Tagore** (Bengal, India, 1861–1941)

*Source: Gitanjali (Song Offerings), translated into English by the author, 1912. Public domain worldwide.*

---

On the day when death will knock at thy door what wilt thou offer to him?

Oh, I will set before my guest the full vessel of my life—I will never let him
go with empty hands.

All the sweet vintage of all my autumn days and summer nights, all the earnings
and gleanings of my busy life will I place before him at the close of my days
when death knocks at my door.

---

## Notes

- **Poet:** Rabindranath Tagore, the Bengali poet and Nobel laureate (1913).
- **Country:** Bengal, India.
- **Era:** Early 20th century; Gitanjali was published in English in 1912.
- **Theme:** Sacrifice as a willing gift — the poet who will set before death the full vessel of his life, all his earnings and gleanings, rather than let the guest go with empty hands.
- **Contrast:** Where Tennyson's "Charge of the Light Brigade" (sample1) shows sacrifice as obedience to a command, Tagore's poem shows sacrifice as a free offering. Together they show sacrifice as both the compelled and the chosen.
