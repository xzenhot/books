It was, by every available measure, a triumph of process.

The committee had met for eleven consecutive weeks to discuss the urgent question of the leak in the roof, and had produced, at last, a document of forty-one pages titled "Toward a Framework for the Consideration of the Matter of the Roof." The document did not mention the roof in any actionable sense, but it did establish, with commendable rigor, that the question was a complex one, and that complexity, as everyone agreed, must be respected.

The rain, meanwhile, had been briefed on the schedule. It declined to comply, which was itself taken as evidence of the depth of the problem, and of the wisdom of further study.

When the roof finally fell in, it was decided that the fault lay not with any individual, but with the weather, which had acted unilaterally and without regard for due process. A new committee was formed at once, to consider the matter of the weather. Its first act was to order the minutes of the previous committee, which had been destroyed by water.

No one was blamed. Everyone was thanked. The rain continued, as it always does, to ignore the framework entirely.
