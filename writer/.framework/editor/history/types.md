1. Structural Metadata (Form & Prosody)

This classification captures the physical architecture and mathematical constraints of the poem.
• Form Type: Categorical attribute. (e.g., Sonnet, Haiku, Villanelle, Ghazal, Rubaiyat, Free Verse, Blank Verse, Prose Poem).
• Stanzaic Structure: Numeric/Categorical array. Type and count of stanzas (e.g., Couplet, Tercet, Quatrain, Sestet, Octave).
• Meter Type: Categorical attribute. The rhythmic foot pattern (e.g., Iambic, Trochaic, Anapestic, Dactylic).
• Line Metric Count: Categorical attribute. Number of feet per line (e.g., Pentameter, Tetrameter, Hexameter).
• Rhyme Scheme: String pattern. The formulaic representation of terminal rhyme (e.g., ABAB, AABB, ABBA, AABA).

2. Historical & Cultural Metadata (Era & Geography)

This taxonomy places the text in a physical location and a specific point in human history.
• Chronological Era: Temporal category. (e.g., Classical Antiquity, Medieval, Renaissance, Enlightenment, Victorian, Modernist, Postmodern, Contemporary).
• Date of Composition/Publication: ISO Date / Year. (e.g., 1922).
• Geocultural Origin: Geographical taxonomy. The region, country, or empire where the work originated (e.g., Persian, Anglo-Saxon, Bengali, Tang Dynasty, Greco-Roman).
• Language & Dialect: ISO Language Code. (e.g., en-US, bn, fa).

3. Philosophical & Aesthetic Metadata (Movement & School)

This category captures the collective artistic alignment, manifestos, or intellectual revolutions the poem belongs to.
• Literary Movement: Categorical attribute. Broad intellectual shifts (e.g., Romanticism, Transcendentalism, Surrealism, Symbolism, Beat Generation, Imagism).
• School / Collective: Categorical attribute. Specific, tightly-knit groups of contemporary poets (e.g., Lake Poets, New York School, Confessional Poets, Hungry Generation).

4. Stylistic & Linguistic Metadata (Poetic Voice)

This classification models the internal linguistic mechanics, voice choices, and tone configurations of the specific text or author.
• Diction Level: Ordinal scale. (e.g., Archaic, High/Formal, Colloquial, Vernacular, Slang).
• Tone / Mood Profile: Array of string attributes. (e.g., Melancholic, Elegiac, Satirical, Whimsical, Exultant, Sombre).
• Dominant Literary Devices: Array of string attributes. Rhetorical tools heavily leveraged in the syntax (e.g., Alliteration, Assonance, Enjambment, Caesura, Extended Metaphor, Polysyndeton).

5. Content & Thematic Metadata (Subject Matter)

This classification is used for thematic discovery, semantic tagging, and semantic search mapping.
• Macro Theme: Broad categorical tag. (e.g., Mortality, Nature, Political Resistance, Devotion/Spirituality, Alienation, Romance, Memory).
• Subject Tags: Granular keyword array. (e.g., war, autumn, monastic-life, railways, grief).
• Poetic Mode: Categorical attribute. The high-level intent of the narrative delivery (e.g., Lyric, Narrative, Epic, Dramatic Monologue).

Part I: The Architectural Blueprint (What is Built)


1. Form & Geometry

• Definition: The foundational matrix, shape, and physical casing chosen for the text.
• Variations: Strict Classical Blueprints (Sonnet, Ghazal, Villanelle, Sestina) versus Open Spatial Structures (Organic Free Verse, Prose Poetry, Concrete/Shape verse).

2. Rhymes & Echoes

• Definition: The mathematical design of terminal and internal acoustic sound-matching.
• Variations: Perfect End-Rhymes (ABAB/AABB), Assonance/Vowel Echoes, Consonance/Syllabic friction, Slant/Imperfect Rhymes, and Head-Rhyme/Alliteration.

3. Metre & Prosody

• Definition: The calculated rhythm, time signature, and arrangement of accented versus unaccented syllables.
• Variations: Rigid Foot Metres (Iambic Pentameter, Trochaic Tetrameter) versus Fluctuating Speech Cadences (Accentual Verse, Variable Foot, Syncopated/Jazz rhythm).

4. White Space & Spatial Typography

• Definition: How the text uses the empty space of the canvas to create visual breathing room, timing, and pauses.
• Variations: Left-Aligned Blocks, Stepped/Staircase drop lines, Dispersed/Scattered text fields, and Caesura gaps (medial breathing breaks inside a line).

Part II: The Lexical Engine (How it Speaks)


5. Word Selection (Diction Register)

• Definition: The exact tier, tone, and historical lineage of the vocabulary drafted into the poem.
• Variations: High-Hieratic (Academic, liturgical, elevated archaic words) to Demotic/Vernacular (Slang, conversational speech, local idioms, and technical jargon).

6. Imagery Profiles (Sensory Axes)

• Definition: The deliberate channeling of vocabulary to trigger the five human senses.
• Variations: Concrete/Visual (Tangible objects), Auditory (Soundscapes), Tactile/Kinesthetic (Texture and raw movement), and Abstract/Surrealist (Dream-logic and philosophical frameworks).

7. Comparative Devices (Metaphor & Trope)

• Definition: The imaginative machinery used to connect two unrelated realities to spark new meaning.
• Variations: Direct Similes, Deep Metaphors, Conceits (Extended structural metaphors spanning whole stanzas), and Synesthesia (blending senses, like "hearing a color").

8. Rhetorical Syntax

• Definition: The deliberate manipulation of standard grammar rules to force emphasis, speed, or drama.
• Variations: Hyperbaton (Inverting standard word order for rhyme/meter), Anaphora (Repeating starting phrases), and Asyndeton (Stripping out conjunctions to accelerate pacing).

Part III: Identity, Context & Spirit (Who & What)


9. Signature (The Poet’s Voiceprint)

• Definition: The unmistakable, recurring artistic DNA, idiosyncrasies, and stylistic ticks unique to an individual creator across their entire body of work.
• Variations: Typographic Signatures (like Emily Dickinson's intense dash use), Lexical Signatures (a unique obsession with specific words), and Tone Signatures.

10. Subjects (The Narrative Anchor)

• Definition: The immediate, literal canvas or plot element being depicted—the raw material of the text.
• Variations: Natural Landscapes, Historical/Political Events, Direct Human Portraits, Found Objects, and Autobiographical Incidents.

11. Theme (The Universal Core)

• Definition: The underlying philosophical, existential, or emotional argument that the Subject is being used to explore.
• Variations: Mortality/Time, Alienation/Isolation, The Sublime/Devotion, Political Resistance, and Romantic Desire.

12. Style (The Aesthetic School)

• Definition: The high-level execution profile that characterizes the manner of expression.
• Variations: Minimalist/Distilled (Stark, tight, no decoration), Baroque/Ornamental (Dense, heavily adjectives, complex), Confessional (Unmasked raw emotion), or Language-Centric (Sound over literal meaning).

Part IV: Methods of Poetry Writing (How it is Made)


13. Generative Methods (The First Spark)

• Definition: The operational method used by the artist to capture or pull raw text out of thin air.
• Variations:
	• Automatic Writing: Fast, unedited stream-of-consciousness capture.
	• Ekphrasis: Composing a poem in direct response to a piece of visual art (a painting or sculpture).
	• Found/Cento Methods: Harvesting, cutting, and collaging words out of existing newspapers, books, or external texts.

14. Structural Execution Methods

• Definition: The framework utilized to organize thoughts into verses during the creation process.
• Variations: Narrative Staging (Telling a linear story), Lyric Outburst (Capturing a singular emotional moment), and Dialectical/Argumentative (Setting up a debate between stanzas).

15. Constraint-Driven Methods (Oulipo Frameworks)

• Definition: Intentionally applying strict, arbitrary rules to break creative blocks and force unexpected word combinations.
• Variations: Lipograms (Writing without using a specific vowel like 'E'), Abecedarian (Each line starting with the next letter of the alphabet), and fixed-syllable counts.

16. Revision & Compression Methods (The Polish)

• Definition: The critical action phase where the raw draft is edited down into a finished art piece.
• Variations: Radical Slicing (Stripping down syntax to create a chiseled look), Sonic Re-tuning (Swapping out words solely to smooth the rhythm), and Stanzaic Re-shaping (Changing where the lines break to shift the visual weight).
