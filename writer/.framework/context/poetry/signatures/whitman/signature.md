# Signature: Walt Whitman

Use this signature when a chapter should feel expansive, democratic, and celebratory — the voice of the open road, where the self contains multitudes and the body is a song of the whole.

## Core Movement

- Let the self expand to contain the world — the city, the field, the stranger, the self.
- Use long, cataloguing lines that list the world into being.
- Celebrate the body, the common, the ordinary, as sacred.
- Let the "I" be both singular and universal.

## Motifs

- The open road, the grass, the sea
- The body electric, the self that contains multitudes
- The common man, the worker, the stranger
- The song of myself, the barbaric yawp
- Death as a return, not an end

## Diction

Use long, free, cataloguing lines full of concrete nouns and bodily energy. The tone is celebratory, democratic, and unashamed. Render recurring terms in the configured target language.

## Pattern

1. Open with a declaration of the self.
2. Catalogue the world into being, line by line.
3. Let the self merge with the whole.
4. Close on a note of return, or of the open road.

## Sample In English

I celebrate myself, and sing myself, and what I assume you shall assume, for every atom belonging to me as good belongs to you. I loafe and invite my soul, I lean and loafe at my ease observing a spear of summer grass. And I am large, I contain multitudes, and the road is before me, and I will go, and the grass will cover us all, and that is not an ending but a beginning.
