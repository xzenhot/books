The room smelled of old wood and wet wool, and the light came through the single window at a low angle, turning the dust in the air to slow, drifting gold.

Along the far wall the shelves leaned slightly, and on them the books had settled into each other like sleeping animals, their spines cracked and softened by damp. A clock on the mantel kept its own time, its tick uneven, as if it had long ago given up on keeping pace with the world outside.

The floor was cold underfoot, worn smooth in the middle where feet had crossed for years, darker at the edges where no one walked. In the corner, a chair faced the window, its cushion hollowed into the shape of the one who used to sit there, and on its arm a cup still held the ghost of tea, a brown ring where the liquid had once been.

From the next room came the faint sound of water moving through pipes, a sound like the house breathing in its sleep. And the light went on falling, patient, through the dust, filling the quiet with the color of an afternoon that had nowhere to go.
