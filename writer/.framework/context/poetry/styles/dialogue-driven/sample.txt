"You're leaving, then."
"I didn't say that."
"You didn't have to. Your shoes are by the door."
She looked at them. Brown. Scuffed at the heel. He was right; she had placed them there at dawn, as if the house were already deciding for her.
"It's not the shoes," she said.
"What is it, then?"
Silence. The kettle clicked off in the kitchen and neither of them went to it.
"It's the silence," she said. "The one that sits in this room every night after you sleep. I keep listening to it like it's going to say your name."
He opened his mouth. Closed it.
"Say something," she said.
"What do you want me to say?"
"That you hear it too."
"I hear it."
"Then why do you never—"
"Because I'm afraid of it," he said. "There. Is that what you wanted?"
The door stood between them, waiting to be opened.
