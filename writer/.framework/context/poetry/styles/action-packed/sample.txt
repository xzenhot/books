The door splintered.

He was already moving. Down the corridor, boots slamming the boards, the wall a blur. Behind him a shout, a shot, a lamp knocked spinning. Glass. The stairs took him two at a time, and the railing shrieked under his grip.

Cold air hit his face. The alley. Rain slick on the stones. He turned left, lungs burning, and the sound of running feet doubled behind him, closer now, too close.

A crate. He vaulted it. A wire. He ducked. The fence rose out of the dark and he hit it at a run, fingers catching the wet metal, hauling himself up and over, and the drop on the far side jarred through his knees and up into his teeth.

The train horn split the night. Ten seconds. He could see the lights now, sliding toward the station, and the platform was a hundred meters of wet concrete between him and it.

He ran. The horn again. The gap closing. He threw himself forward as the doors began to slide, and his hand found the rail, and the train pulled him into the dark and the rain and the roar, and left the shouting far behind, swallowed whole by the night.
