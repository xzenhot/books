# Desh Style — Corpus Analysis (Vaishnav / Mendicant Register)

A lexical, syntactic, and structural analysis of the Vaishnav-mendicant voice that
mirrors the tantric `analysis.md`. This is the **vaishnav re-keying** of the same
corpus habits: the *surface mechanics* (word recurrence, sentence build, SOV bend,
unit length) stay the same, but every image family is re-derived from the world of
the **sadhu**, the **boiragi**, the **bostumi**, and the **fakir** — the renunciants,
the village minstrels, the *kirtan* singers, the wanderers of the *akhra* and the
*mazar*.

> **Scope note.** As with `analysis.md`, these are close-reading counts, not a
> machine tokenizer. Bengali tokenizes on word boundaries, and the mendicant register
> mixes *sadhubhasha*, *brijabuli*-flavored devotional idiom, and everyday village
> speech. Counts describe *tendency* — which is what a writer needs.

---

## 1. Word Usage & Frequency

### 1.1 The highest-frequency nouns and their families

The register orbits a small, closed devotional vocabulary. The same roots repeat;
the repetition *is* the method.

| Word / root | Rough recurrence | Family |
|---|---|---|
| নাম (the Name) | highest — nearly every poem | Devotion / the Name |
| হরি / কৃষ্ণ / রাধা / নিমাই (deity names) | high | Devotion / the Name |
| সাধু / বৈরাগী / বোষ্টমী (renunciant figures) | high | Mendicant order |
| ফকির / দরবেশ / মাজার (fakir / dervish / shrine) | high | Mendicant order |
| প্রেম / ভক্তি / রস (love / devotion / essence) | high | Devotion |
| মাটি / গঙ্গা / জল (earth / Ganga / water) | recurring | The land |
| খোল / করতাল / একতারা (drum / cymbals / one-string) | high | Kirtan & song |
| ভিক্ষা / ঝোলা / ভেক (alms / cloth-bag / garb) | high | Renunciation |
| প্রাণ / দেহ / বুক (breath / body / chest) | high | The dissolving body |
| কীর্তন / গান / পদ (kirtan / song / verse) | high | Song & poetry |
| সাঁই / পীর / গুরুমশাই (lord / pir / master) | recurring | The master |

**Key finding:** the register does not reach for a wide dictionary. It *re-keys a
narrow set of roots into many grammatical forms* (নাম → নামগান → নামরস; প্রেম →
প্রেমগাথা → প্রেমানন্দ). This is a closed, incantatory devotional vocabulary.

### 1.2 Function-word rhythm

- **"হরি বলো…" (call Hari's name…)** and **"ও ভাই…" / "ও মন…" (O brother… / O
  mind…)** are the dominant anaphoric engines — the direct address of the *kirtan*
  singer to a wandering audience.
- **"কেন…?" (why…?)** and **"কী পাব…?" (what shall I gain…?)** open the questions the
  voice never resolves.
- **"নামের স্রোতে…" (in the current of the Name)** is the recurring locative of
  surrender.

### 1.3 Rare/loaded (*tatsama*) density

The register is dense in devotional Sanskritic and Perso-Arabic abstractions: **প্রেম,
ভক্তি, রস, মাধুর্য, অভিসার, সখীভাব, প্রপত্তি** (Sanskritic) alongside **ফকির, দরবেশ,
সাঁই, খোদা, বন্দেগি** (Perso-Arabic). Rough density is **25–40%** in elevated
passages — a little lower than the tantric register, because the plain village word
(the dust, the footpath, the *jhola*) carries more of the weight.

## 2. Sequences & Distance (N-gram / juxtaposition habits)

### 2.1 The dominant two-term collision pattern

The signature move is a **two-term juxtaposition where the sacred and the low sit
together in one breath** — the god's name against the bare foot, the *mazar* against
the hunger of the road:

| Collision | Register |
|---|---|
| "নামের স্রোতে মাটির ঘর" (the current of the Name, the mud hut) | sadhu |
| "ভিক্ষার ঝোলায় রাধার প্রেম" (Radha's love in the alms-bag) | bostumi |
| "মাজারের ধুলো, পায়ের তলা" (the shrine's dust, the sole of the foot) | fakir |
| "খোল বাজে, পেট খালি" (the drum beats, the belly empty) | kirtan |

**Distance finding:** the two colliding terms sit **immediately adjacent or one clause
apart, with zero bridge**. The leap — from the god's name to the empty belly — is
bare; there is no connective.

### 2.2 The anaphoric chain (repetition as structure)

- The voice builds on **"হরি বলো… হরি বলো…"** and **"নাম… নাম… নাম…"** — a litany
  that accumulates and then breaks into a single quiet line of surrender.
- The boiragi's refrain **"ও মন, কী পাবি ভিক্ষা ক'রে…"** repeats at clause heads with
  short gaps, one breath.

**Distance finding:** the repeated head sits at the *start of successive clauses*; the
gap is short, so the litany reads as one accumulating breath.

### 2.3 The circular return (long-range repetition)

The kirtan opens and closes on the same refrain — "নাম রইল, আমি রইলাম না" (the Name
remains, I do not) — so the whole poem is framed by a single repeated image at
maximal distance.

## 3. Parts of Speech (Heuristic)

### 3.1 Nouns and nominalizations dominate

- **Verb-turned-nouns and adjective-turned-nouns are frequent** — the register prefers
  the *state* over the *act*: "ভক্তির পথ" (the path of devotion), "নামের স্রোত" (the
  current of the Name), "প্রেমানন্দ" (the bliss of love), "বৈরাগ্য" (renunciation).

### 3.2 Verbs are short, present, and often dropped

- Finite verbs tend to be **short and in the present habitual or imperative**:
  "বলে", "গায়", "চলে", "ডাকে", "ভিক্ষা করে".
- In the most incantatory lines the verb is **elided**, leaving bare noun phrases:
  "ও মন! নাম।" (no verb), "মাটির ঘর, গঙ্গার জল।"

### 3.3 Adjectives cluster before the noun

- Pre-nominal modification is common: "অভাগা বৈরাগী", "শ্যামল নিমাই", "নীলা রাধার
  আঁচল". The pile-up is a density device, softer than the tantric register's.

### 3.4 Pronouns: a fixed first-person "আমি" and a vocative "ও মন/ও ভাই"

- "আমি" is the constant subject; "ও মন" (O mind) and "ও ভাই" (O brother) are the
  recurring vocative addressees; "কী" (what) and "কেন" (why) are the recurring
  interrogatives. Everything is first-person or direct address.

## 4. Subject–Verb–Object Order

Bengali is canonically **SOV**. The register mostly honors it, but bends it in two
signature ways:

### 4.1 Base SOV holds

- "ভিক্ষা [O] পেটে [O] নিলাম [V]" — object(s) precede the verb.
- "নামের ঝোলা [O] কাঁধে [O] ঝুলিয়ে [V] চলে গেলাম।" — object before the verb.

### 4.2 Verb-final dropped → noun-final fragments

Verbs are frequently elided, so many "sentences" are **noun-final fragments**:

- "ও মন! নাম।" — vocative, then bare noun.
- "মাটির ঘর, গঙ্গার জল।" — bare noun pairs, no verb.

**Order finding:** the most characteristic "sentence" is **S + complement (no verb)**
— a noun that closes the line without a predicate. This is the engine of surrender.

### 4.3 Fronting for invocation

- "নামের স্রোতে আমি ভেসে গেলাম—" — locative-led.
- "মাজারের ধুলোয় মাথা ঠেকিয়ে—" — locative-led, verb deferred.

## 5. Sentence Length & Rhythm

### 5.1 The two-sentence economy

1. **Long, winding, enjambed sentences** — the kirtan's breath, **15–25+ words**,
   clauses piling and turning.
2. **Short, flat fragments** — **2–6 words**, sometimes one word: "নাম।", "ও মন!",
   "ভিক্ষা।"

**Length finding:** a long breath followed by a short snap — the long sentence builds
the longing; the short sentence lands the surrender.

### 5.2 Approximate per-poem sentence-length profile

| Register shape | Dominant sentence shape | Typical words/sentence |
|---|---|---|
| sadhu (renunciant) | long incantatory, one-word close | 12–25, ending 1 |
| boiragi (wandering monk) | anaphoric refrain, short close | 8–20, with 3–4 fragments |
| bostumi (female devotee) | short, tender, fragmentary | 4–12 |
| fakir (mendicant) | long, breath-heavy, then snap | 12–20 |
| kirtan (song) | line-broken, short-to-medium | 5–12 |

### 5.3 Punctuation as rhythm

- The **dash (–)**, **question mark (?)**, and **exclamation (!)** carry the voice —
  questions of longing ("কী পাব?"), exclamations of the Name ("হরি!").
- The full stop cuts rather than concludes, snapping the long breath into a fragment.

---

## 6. Summary of Findings (the reproducible texture — vaishnav register)

1. **Vocabulary** is closed and devotional: the Name, the deity, the mendicant
   figures (sadhu, boiragi, bostumi, fakir), the kirtan's instruments, the alms and
   the road. *Tatsama* + Perso-Arabic density is high in elevated passages.
2. **Collision** is the core device: the sacred Name and the low (mud hut, empty
   belly, bare foot) sit adjacent with **no connective bridge**.
3. **Anaphora** ("হরি বলো…", "নাম… নাম…") and **circular return** provide long-range
   structure; repetition is the engine.
4. **Parts of speech** skew to nouns and nominalizations; verbs are short, present,
   and often dropped; adjectives pile pre-nominally; "আমি" and the vocative "ও মন /
   ও ভাই" carry the address.
5. **Word order** is base SOV, but the signature "sentence" is **subject + complement
   with no verb** — a noun-final fragment of surrender.
6. **Sentence length** alternates long (15–25 words) and short (2–6 words); poems often
   close on a single word ("নাম।") or a short flat clause.
7. **Punctuation** (dash, question, exclamation) carries rhythm; the full stop cuts
   rather than concludes.
