The old woman sat on the stoop with a bowl of unshelled peas in her lap, and the morning passed over her like a slow tide.

She did not look up when the bus went by, or when the dog crossed the street with something in its mouth. Her thumb found the seam of each pod and split it, and the peas dropped into the bowl with a small, clean sound, one after another, as if the day were being counted out.

Her son's window above stayed shut. It had been shut for three days. She knew this the way she knew the weight of the bowl in her lap, and she did not hurry.

When the bowl was full she set it down and looked at her hands, which were old now, and still. The peas waited, green and small and patient. And for a long moment she simply sat, listening to the street, to the shut window, to the morning that kept arriving and asking nothing.
