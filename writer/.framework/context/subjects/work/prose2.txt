# Prose Sample 2 — Work

## The Prophet (On Work)

**By Kahlil Gibran** (Lebanon, 1883–1931)

*Source: The Prophet (1923). Public domain worldwide.*

---

Then a ploughman said, Speak to us of Work.

And he answered, saying:

You work that you may keep pace with the earth and the soul of the earth.

For to be idle is to become a stranger unto the seasons, and to step out of
life's procession, that marches in majesty and proud submission towards the
infinite.

When you work you are a flute through whose heart the whispering of the hours
turns to music.

Which of you would be a reed, dumb and silent, when all else sings together in
unison?

Always you have been told that work is a curse and labour a misfortune.

But I say to you that when you work you fulfil a part of earth's furthest
dream, assigned to you when that dream was born,

And in keeping yourself with labour you are in truth loving life,

And to love life through labour is to be intimate with life's inmost secret.

---

## Notes

- **Writer:** Kahlil Gibran, the Lebanese-American poet and philosopher.
- **Country:** Lebanon (writing in English, from the United States).
- **Era:** Early 20th century; The Prophet was published in 1923.
- **Theme:** Work as a fulfilment of life — to work is to keep pace with the earth, to be a flute through which the hours turn to music, and to love life through labour.
- **Contrast:** Where Steinbeck's Grapes of Wrath (prose1) shows work as struggle and loss, Gibran's prose lifts work into a spiritual calling. Together they show work as both burden and blessing.
