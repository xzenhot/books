# Sample 2 — Solitude

## Gitanjali 18

**By Rabindranath Tagore** (Bengal, India, 1861–1941)

*Source: Gitanjali (Song Offerings), translated into English by the author, 1912. Public domain worldwide.*

---

Clouds heap upon clouds and it darkens. Ah, love, why dost thou let me wait
outside at the door all alone?

In the busy moments of the noontide work I am with the crowd, but on this dark
lonely day it is only for thee that I hope.

If thou showest me not thy face, if thou leavest me wholly aside, I know not
how I am to pass these long, rainy hours.

I keep gazing on the far-away gloom of the sky, and my heart wanders wailing
with the restless wind.

---

## Notes

- **Poet:** Rabindranath Tagore, the Bengali poet and Nobel laureate (1913).
- **Country:** Bengal, India.
- **Era:** Early 20th century; Gitanjali was published in English in 1912.
- **Theme:** Solitude as longing — the soul left alone at the door on a dark, rainy day, waiting for the beloved, its heart wandering with the restless wind.
- **Contrast:** Where Yeats's "Innisfree" (sample1) seeks solitude as a peace, Tagore's poem feels solitude as an ache — the loneliness of waiting. Together they show solitude as both the refuge and the wound.
