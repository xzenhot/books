# Prose Sample 1 — God

## The Brothers Karamazov

**By Fyodor Dostoevsky** (Russia, 1821–1881)

*Source: The Brothers Karamazov (1880), translated by Constance Garnett. Public domain worldwide.*

---

"Listen: if everyone must suffer, in order to buy eternal harmony with their
suffering, pray tell me what have children got to do with it? It's quite
incomprehensible why they should have to suffer, and why they should buy harmony with
their suffering. Why should they too furnish material to enrich the soil for the
harmony of the future? I understand solidarity in sin among men; I understand
solidarity in retribution, too; but there can be no such solidarity with
children. And if it is really true that they must share responsibility for all their
fathers' crimes, surely that truth is not of this world and is not
comprehensible to me."

...

"I renounce the higher harmony altogether. It's not worth the tears of that one
tortured child who beat itself on the breast with its little fist and prayed in
its stench to 'dear, kind God'! It's not worth it, because those tears are
unatoned for. They must be atoned for, or there can be no harmony. But how, how are
you going to atone for them? Is it possible? By their being avenged? But what do
I care for avenging them? What do I care for a hell for oppressors? What good can
hell do, since those children have already been tortured?"

---

## Notes

- **Writer:** Fyodor Dostoevsky, the Russian novelist and religious thinker.
- **Country:** Russia.
- **Era:** Late 19th century; the novel was published in 1880.
- **Theme:** The problem of evil — Ivan Karamazov's rebellion against a God who would permit the suffering of innocent children, and his refusal of any harmony bought at such a price.
- **Contrast:** Where the poems in this subject address God in faith and longing, Dostoevsky's prose gives voice to doubt and rebellion. It shows that the question of God is also the question of suffering.
