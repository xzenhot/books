# Prose Sample 1 — Love

## Pride and Prejudice

**By Jane Austen** (England, 1775–1817)

*Source: Pride and Prejudice (1813). Public domain worldwide.*

---

It is a truth universally acknowledged, that a single man in possession of a
good fortune, must be in want of a wife.

However little known the feelings or views of such a man may be on his first
entering a neighbourhood, this truth is so well fixed in the minds of the
surrounding families, that he is considered as the rightful property of some one or
other of their daughters.

"My dear Mr. Bennet," said his lady to him one day, "have you heard that
Netherfield Park is let at last?"

Mr. Bennet replied that he had not.

"But it is," returned she; "for Mrs. Long has just been here, and she told me
all about it."

Mr. Bennet made no answer.

"Do you not want to know who has taken it?" cried his wife impatiently.

"You want to tell me, and I have no objection to hearing it."

This was invitation enough.

---

## Notes

- **Writer:** Jane Austen, the English novelist of manners and the heart.
- **Country:** England.
- **Era:** Regency England; the novel was published in 1813.
- **Theme:** Love entangled with society — how marriage, money, and pride shape the path of the heart, and how true love must learn to see past first impressions.
- **Contrast:** Where the poems in this subject speak of love in the abstract and the divine, Austen grounds love in the everyday comedy of courtship, family, and social expectation.
