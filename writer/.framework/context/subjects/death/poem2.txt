# Sample 2 — Death

## Gitanjali 95

**By Rabindranath Tagore** (Bengal, India, 1861–1941)

*Source: Gitanjali (Song Offerings), translated into English by the author, 1912. Public domain worldwide.*

---

I was not aware of the moment when I first crossed the threshold of this life.

What was the power that made me open out into this vast mystery like a bud in
the forest at midnight!

When in the morning I looked upon the light I felt in a moment that I was no
stranger in this world, that the inscrutable without name and form had taken me
in its arms in the form of my own mother.

Even so, in death the same unknown will appear as ever known to me. And because
I love this life, I know I shall love death as well.

The child cries out when from the right breast the mother takes it away, in the
very next moment to find in the left one its consolation.

---

## Notes

- **Poet:** Rabindranath Tagore, the Bengali poet and Nobel laureate (1913).
- **Country:** Bengal, India.
- **Era:** Early 20th century; Gitanjali was published in English in 1912.
- **Theme:** Death as a return to the known — the same unknown that received us at birth will receive us at death, and because we love life, we shall love death as well.
- **Contrast:** Where Dickinson's poem (sample1) rides with Death as a courteous stranger, Tagore greets death as a familiar, even a mother. Together they show death met without fear, in two different keys.
