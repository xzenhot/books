# Sample 2 — Hope

## Gitanjali 37

**By Rabindranath Tagore** (Bengal, India, 1861–1941)

*Source: Gitanjali (Song Offerings), translated into English by the author, 1912. Public domain worldwide.*

---

I thought that my voyage had come to its end at the last limit of my power,—that
the path before me was closed, that provisions were exhausted and the time come
to take shelter in a silent obscurity.

But I find that thy will knows no end in me. And when old words die out on the
tongue, new melodies break forth from the heart; and where the old tracks are
lost, new country is revealed with its wonders.

---

## Notes

- **Poet:** Rabindranath Tagore, the Bengali poet and Nobel laureate (1913).
- **Country:** Bengal, India.
- **Era:** Early 20th century; Gitanjali was published in English in 1912.
- **Theme:** Hope as renewal — when the path seems closed and the provisions exhausted, new melodies break forth and new country is revealed, because the will of the divine knows no end.
- **Contrast:** Where Dickinson's "Hope" (sample1) is a small bird that never stops singing, Tagore's hope is a whole new country revealed at the end of the road. Together they show hope as both the small and the vast.
