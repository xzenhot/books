# Prose Sample 1 — Friendship

## The Three Musketeers

**By Alexandre Dumas** (France, 1802–1870)

*Source: Les Trois Mousquetaires (1844). Public domain worldwide.*

---

The young man, for a moment, remained motionless, as if undecided; then, with
a gesture of resolution, he advanced toward the three musketeers, who were
conversing together.

"Gentlemen," said he, "you will, I trust, excuse me if I interrupt your
conversation; but I have an appointment with one of you, and I am anxious to keep
it."

"With me?" said Athos.

"With you, monsieur. My name is d'Artagnan."

The three musketeers looked at one another, and then at the young man, whose
manner, though bold, was not without a certain grace.

"I am to fight you all three, it appears," said d'Artagnan, "one after the
other. But before we begin, let me say that I have no quarrel with any of you, and
that I would rather have you for friends than for enemies."

"Then you shall have your wish," said Athos, smiling, "for we are not the men
to refuse a brave heart. From this day, we are four, and the four of us are one."

---

## Notes

- **Writer:** Alexandre Dumas, the French novelist of adventure and romance.
- **Country:** France.
- **Era:** Mid-19th century; the novel was published in 1844.
- **Theme:** Friendship forged in a moment — the young d'Artagnan who comes to fight three men and leaves as their brother, bound by the famous oath "all for one, and one for all."
- **Contrast:** Where the poems in this subject reflect on friendship in quiet, Dumas's prose shows friendship born in action and danger. It is friendship as a bond of courage and loyalty.
