# Sample 2 — Nostalgia

## Banalata Sen

**By Jibanananda Das** (Bengal, India, 1899–1954)

*Source: Banalata Sen (1942), translated from the Bengali. Public domain in India.*

---

For thousands of years I roamed the paths of this earth,
From waters round Ceylon in dead of night to Malayan seas.
Much have I wandered. I was there in the gray world of Asoka
And Bimbisara, pressed on through darkness to the city of Vidarbha.
I am a weary heart surrounded by life's frothy ocean.
To me she gave a moment's peace — Banalata Sen of Natore.

Her hair was an ancient darkness of night Vidisha,
Her face, the craftsmanship of Sravasti. As the helmsman,
Rudderless, far out upon the sea, when he glimpses
The grass-green land of a cinnamon isle, so through darkness I saw her.
She raised her eyes and asked, "Where have you been so long?"
And she raised her bird's-nest eyes — Banalata Sen of Natore.

At the day's end, like hush of dew
Comes evening. A hawk wipes the scent of sunlight from its wings.
When all colours take leave from the world except for the flicker
Of the hovering fireflies as manuscript is painted with tales,
All birds come home, all rivers, all of this life's transactions finished.
Only darkness remains, and, face to face with me, Banalata Sen.

---

## Notes

- **Poet:** Jibanananda Das, the great Bengali modernist poet.
- **Country:** Bengal, India.
- **Era:** Mid-20th century; the poem was published in 1942.
- **Theme:** Nostalgia as a longing for a lost, dreamlike home — the weary wanderer who finds, in the memory of a woman and a place, a moment of peace at the end of all his wandering.
- **Contrast:** Where Wordsworth's daffodils (sample1) return as a remembered joy, Jibanananda Das's nostalgia is deeper and more melancholy — a longing for a peace that belongs to another time and place.
