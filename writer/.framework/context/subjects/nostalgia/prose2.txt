# Prose Sample 2 — Nostalgia

## Pather Panchali (Song of the Road)

**By Bibhutibhushan Bandyopadhyay** (Bengal, India, 1894–1950)

*Source: Pather Panchali (1929), a Bengali novel. Public domain in India; translated into many languages.*

---

The road ran on, past the bamboo grove and the old banyan tree, and out into
the open fields where the mustard bloomed yellow in the winter sun. Apu and Durga
had walked this road a hundred times, and yet it was never the same road twice.
In the rains it was a river of mud; in summer it was dust that rose in little
clouds about their feet; and in winter it lay still and golden, as if it were
waiting for them.

Their mother, Sarbajaya, stood at the door of the house and watched them go.
She did not call them back. She only stood there, one hand shading her eyes, until
the two small figures had dwindled into the distance and the road was empty
again.

Years later, when Apu was a man and far from home, it was this road he
remembered — not the cities he had seen, not the faces of strangers, but this one
road, yellow with mustard, and his sister running ahead of him, and his mother
standing at the door.

---

## Notes

- **Writer:** Bibhutibhushan Bandyopadhyay, the Bengali novelist whose Pather Panchali became the basis of Satyajit Ray's celebrated film.
- **Country:** Bengal, India.
- **Era:** Early 20th century; the novel was published in 1929.
- **Theme:** The nostalgia of the lost village — the road, the fields, the mother at the door, remembered from exile as the true home of the heart.
- **Contrast:** Where Proust's madeleine (prose1) unlocks memory through a single taste, Bibhutibhushan's nostalgia is for a whole landscape — a place and a childhood that can never be returned to.
