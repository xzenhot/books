# Prose Sample 2 — Childhood

## Pather Panchali (Song of the Road)

**By Bibhutibhushan Bandyopadhyay** (Bengal, India, 1894–1950)

*Source: Pather Panchali (1929), a Bengali novel. Public domain in India; translated into many languages.*

---

Apu was born in the village of Nishchindipur, in a house that stood at the edge
of a bamboo grove. His first memory was of light — the light that came through
the leaves in the morning, and the light on his mother's face as she bent over
him.

He grew up in that house, and in the fields around it, and in the company of
his sister Durga, who was older and knew everything. She knew where the wild
berries grew, and where the birds nested, and how to make a whistle from a green
mango leaf. She knew the names of the stars, and the stories that went with them.

Together they roamed the village and the fields, and the days were long and
full. Apu did not know then that the world was wide, or that the road led
anywhere at all. The road was only the road, and it led to the fields, and the fields
led back to the house, and the house was the whole world.

---

## Notes

- **Writer:** Bibhutibhushan Bandyopadhyay, the Bengali novelist whose Pather Panchali became the basis of Satyajit Ray's celebrated film.
- **Country:** Bengal, India.
- **Era:** Early 20th century; the novel was published in 1929.
- **Theme:** The first world of childhood — the village, the fields, the sister who knows everything, and the house that is the whole world before the road leads away.
- **Contrast:** Where Dickens's David Copperfield (prose1) begins with the child's birth and its omens, Bibhutibhushan begins with the child's first memory of light. Together they show childhood as both a beginning and a whole world.
