# Sample 2 — Philosophy

## Gitanjali 35

**By Rabindranath Tagore** (Bengal, India, 1861–1941)

*Source: Gitanjali (Song Offerings), translated into English by the author, 1912. Public domain worldwide.*

---

Where the mind is without fear and the head is held high;
Where knowledge is free;
Where the world has not been broken up into fragments
By narrow domestic walls;
Where words come out from the depth of truth;
Where tireless striving stretches its arms towards perfection;
Where the clear stream of reason has not lost its way
Into the dreary desert sand of dead habit;
Where the mind is led forward by thee
Into ever-widening thought and action—
Into that heaven of freedom, my Father, let my country awake.

---

## Notes

- **Poet:** Rabindranath Tagore, the Bengali poet and Nobel laureate (1913).
- **Country:** Bengal, India.
- **Era:** Early 20th century; Gitanjali was published in English in 1912.
- **Theme:** The ideal of freedom — a vision of a world without fear, where knowledge is free and reason is not lost in habit, offered as a prayer for the awakening of a nation.
- **Contrast:** Where Khayyám's Rubáiyát (sample1) resigns itself to fate, Tagore's poem is a call to transcend it — philosophy as hope and aspiration rather than acceptance.
