# Prose Sample 1 — Mother

## The Mother

**By Maxim Gorky** (Russia, 1868–1936)

*Source: The Mother (1906–1907), translated from the Russian. Public domain worldwide.*

---

Every day the factory whistle bellowed forth its shrill, roaring, trembling
voices into the smoke-begrimed and greasy atmosphere of the workingmen's suburb;
and obedient to the summons of the power of steam, people poured out of little
gray houses into the street. With somber faces they hastened forward, like
frightened roaches, their muscles stiff from insufficient sleep. In the chill
morning twilight they walked through the narrow, unpaved street to the tall stone
cage that waited for them with cold assurance, illumining their muddy road with
scores of greasy yellow square eyes.

The mud plashed under their feet. Hoarse exclamations of sleepy voices were
heard. Rudely abusive words tore the air. And other sounds floated to meet them —
the heavy whir of machinery, the dissatisfied snort of steam. Tall black chimneys
stretched up into the sky, grim and stern, like thick sticks.

In the evening, when the sun was setting, and its red rays languidly gleamed on
the windows of the houses, the factory ejected its people like burned-out ashes
from its stone bowels, and again they walked through the streets, with black,
smoke-covered faces, radiating the sticky smell of machine oil, and showing the
gleam of hungry teeth.

---

## Notes

- **Writer:** Maxim Gorky, the Russian and Soviet writer, founder of socialist realism.
- **Country:** Russia.
- **Era:** Early 20th century; the novel was written in 1906–1907.
- **Theme:** The mother as witness and conscience — Pelageya, the working-class mother who is drawn into her son's revolutionary struggle and grows from fear into courage.
- **Contrast:** Where the poems in this subject dwell on the mother's tenderness, Gorky's prose places the mother inside a world of labor and oppression, showing how a mother's love can become a force for change.
