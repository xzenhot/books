My neighbor has decided to keep bees. This is, she assures me, a profound act of environmental stewardship, a small rebellion against the modern world, and a return to something ancient and true. She says this while standing in front of a hive that, as far as I can tell, the bees use mainly to organize raids on her kitchen.

The bees, for their part, have not read the literature on stewardship. They have, however, mapped every open window on the block with a precision that would shame a surveyor. I have begun taking my tea indoors, not because I dislike nature, but because nature has developed a particular interest in my sugar bowl.

Yesterday she asked me if I had "come to appreciate the bees." I told her I appreciated them most from behind the glass, at a respectful distance, the way one appreciates the ocean or a difficult relative. She did not laugh. She told me the bees could sense my fear.

I have since learned that bees are not psychic. They cannot sense fear. What they can sense is a man waving a newspaper and running in the general direction of his own front door, which he has forgotten is locked. I can report, with some authority, that they find this extremely interesting.
