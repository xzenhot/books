I have been thinking about the way a door closes behind you, and whether it ever really closes.

There was a house I left once, years ago, and I can still feel the weight of its front door in my hand — the cool of the brass, the small resistance before it clicked into the frame. I stood on the step for a long time, I remember, not because I wanted to go back, but because I could not quite believe that leaving was so simple. One turn of the handle, and a whole life became the past.

And yet here is what I did not understand then: the door did not close. Or rather, it closed on the outside, but something of it stayed open in me. I have carried that house for years now, not as a place I miss, but as a question I have never finished asking — what did I think I was walking toward, when I walked away from everything I knew?

Maybe that is what we mean when we say we cannot go home again. Not that the door is locked, but that the person who would walk through it no longer fits the frame.

I do not have an answer. I am not sure the question even has one. But I have learned to sit with it, the way you sit with an old injury on a cold day — not because it will heal, but because it has become a part of how you know the weather. And some evenings, when a door closes somewhere in the building and the sound reaches me through the walls, I still turn my head, as if I am expecting someone to call my name from the other side.
