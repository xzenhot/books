# Stereotype: Rabindra Sangeet-Inspired Devotional Lyric

Use this stereotype when a chapter should feel songlike, devotional, intimate, and filled with wonder before the beloved, nature, and the unseen order of life.

## Core Movement

- Address an intimate beloved, who may be divine, human, natural, or inward.
- Let longing and union form the two poles of emotion.
- Use refrain-like repetition and gentle questions of wonder.
- Keep the voice tender rather than grandiose.

## Form

- Refrain: the central image or question, returned to after development.
- Verse: a new turn of the thought, image, or emotion.
- Reprise: the refrain returns with deeper meaning.

Render these structural labels in the configured target language when writing in another language.

## Imagery

- Sky, river, wave, wind, flower, cloud, rain, light, lamp, sun, stars
- Heart, breath, dust, feet, pain, joy, separation, union
- Boat, shore, far shore, ferry, garden, storm

## Pattern

1. Choose one core wonder.
2. Shape it into a short refrain.
3. Let each verse unfold one emotional facet.
4. Return to the refrain transformed by what has been revealed.

## Sample In English

Refrain:
By what hidden music does the river move, who can know?
By what unseen hand does the flower open, who can know?

Verse:
When the heart is empty, your wind enters it.
When the lamp is small, your night makes it bright.
I call you by many names and lose them all,
for the one who arrives in silence needs no name.

Reprise:
By what hidden music does the river move, who can know?
By what unseen hand does the heart awaken, who can know?
