# Sample 1 — Politics

## The Rebel

**By Kazi Nazrul Islam** (Bengal, India, 1899–1976)

*Source: "Bidrohi" (The Rebel), 1921, translated from the Bengali. Public domain in India.*

---

Say, Valiant,
Say: High is my head!
Looking at my head
The Himalayan peak bows down its head.

Say, Valiant,
Say: Rending the wide blue sky of the universe,
Leaving behind the moon, the sun, the planets, the stars,
Piercing the earth and the heavens,
Breaking through the throne of God,
I have risen — I, the eternal wonder of the mother-earth.

The angry God of destruction is on my forehead,
Like a royal diadem of victory!

Say, Valiant,
Say: High is my head, ever high!

I am the unutterable grief,
I am the trembling first touch of the virgin,
I am the throbbing tenderness of her first stolen kiss,
I am the fleeting glance of the veiled beloved,
I am her constant surreptitious gaze.

I am the burning volcano in the bosom of the earth,
I am the wild fire of the woods,
I am Hell's mad terrific sea of wrath!

---

## Notes

- **Poet:** Kazi Nazrul Islam, the Bengali poet, musician, and revolutionary, known as the "Rebel Poet" of Bengal.
- **Country:** Bengal, India.
- **Era:** Early 20th century; "Bidrohi" was written in 1921, during the struggle against colonial rule.
- **Theme:** Rebellion as a spiritual force — the poet who declares himself the voice of the oppressed, rising against empire, against God's throne itself, in the name of the downtrodden.
- **Contrast:** This is politics as fire and defiance. It pairs with W. B. Yeats's "Easter, 1916" (sample2), which reflects on the same spirit of rebellion with a quieter, more ambivalent grief.
