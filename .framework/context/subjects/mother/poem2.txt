# Sample 2 — Mother

## To My Mother

**By Edgar Allan Poe** (United States, 1809–1849)

*Source: Published 1849. Public domain worldwide.*

---

Because I feel that, in the Heavens above,
The angels, whispering to one another,
Can find, among their burning terms of love,
None so devotional as that of "Mother,"
Therefore by that dear name I long have called you—
You who are more than mother unto me,
And fill my heart of hearts, where Death installed you
In setting my Virginia's spirit free.
My mother—my own mother, who died early,
Was but the mother of myself; but you
Are mother to the one I loved so dearly,
And thus are dearer than the mother I knew
By that infinity with which my wife
Was dearer to my soul than its soul-life.

---

## Notes

- **Poet:** Edgar Allan Poe, the American poet and master of the macabre.
- **Country:** United States.
- **Era:** Mid-19th century; the poem was published in 1849, the year of Poe's death.
- **Theme:** The mother as the deepest name of love — a mother-in-law who became more than a mother, bound to the poet through the wife he lost.
- **Contrast:** Where Tagore's Gitanjali (sample1) celebrates the mother's joy in the living child, Poe's sonnet is written in grief, to a mother who shares his loss. Together they show the mother's love in its two seasons — the giving and the mourning.
