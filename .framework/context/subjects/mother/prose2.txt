# Prose Sample 2 — Mother

## Srikanta (Part I)

**By Sarat Chandra Chattopadhyay** (Bengal, India, 1876–1938)

*Source: Srikanta (1917), a Bengali novel. Public domain in India; translated into many languages.*

---

I do not remember my mother. She died when I was very young, and of her I have
kept only a single picture: a woman sitting in the evening light, her face turned
toward the door, waiting. Whether she was waiting for my father, or for me, or
for no one at all, I cannot say. But that picture has never left me.

My aunt used to say that I was my mother's shadow, that I would not let her out
of my sight. And yet I have no memory of her voice, no memory of her touch. Only
that one picture, of a woman waiting in the evening light.

Perhaps that is why, all my life, I have been drawn to women who wait — to the
quiet ones, the patient ones, the ones who sit by a door and look out at the road.
I have spent my life trying to find, in other faces, the face I lost before I
could remember it.

---

## Notes

- **Writer:** Sarat Chandra Chattopadhyay, one of the most beloved Bengali novelists of the early twentieth century.
- **Country:** Bengal, India.
- **Era:** Early 20th century; Srikanta was published in 1917.
- **Theme:** The absent mother — the mother lost in childhood who haunts the whole of a life, and the lifelong search for her face in the faces of others.
- **Contrast:** Where Gorky's The Mother (prose1) shows a mother present and fighting, Sarat Chandra's narrator has only a memory of a mother who is gone. Together they show the mother as both presence and absence.
