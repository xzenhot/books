# Sample 2 — Love

## Gitanjali 32

**By Rabindranath Tagore** (Bengal, India, 1861–1941)

*Source: Gitanjali (Song Offerings), translated into English by the author, 1912. Public domain worldwide.*

---

By all means they try to hold me secure who love me in this world.
But it is otherwise with thy love which is greater than theirs, and thou keepest me free.
Lest I forget them they never venture to leave me alone.
But day passes by after day and thou art not seen.

If I call not thee in my prayers, if I keep not thee in my heart,
thy love for me still waits for my love.

---

## Notes

- **Poet:** Rabindranath Tagore, the Bengali poet, novelist, and composer; the first non-European to win the Nobel Prize in Literature (1913).
- **Country:** Bengal, India.
- **Era:** Early 20th century; Gitanjali was published in English in 1912.
- **Theme:** Divine love — a love that does not cling or confine but sets the beloved free, and that waits patiently even when it is not returned.
- **Contrast:** Where Elizabeth Barrett Browning's sonnet (sample1) counts the ways of human love, Tagore's poem turns the same longing toward God. The two together show love's two faces — the human and the divine.
