# Prose Sample 2 — Beauty

## The Prophet (On Beauty)

**By Kahlil Gibran** (Lebanon, 1883–1931)

*Source: The Prophet (1923). Public domain worldwide.*

---

And a poet said, Speak to us of Beauty.

And he answered:

Where shall you seek beauty, and how shall you find her unless she herself be
your way and your guide?

And how shall you speak of her except she be the weaver of your speech?

The aggrieved and the injured say, "Beauty is kind and gentle.

Like a young mother half-shy of her own glory she walks among us."

And the passionate say, "Nay, beauty is a thing of might and dread.

Like the tempest she shakes the earth beneath us and the sky above us."

The tired and the weary say, "Beauty is of soft whisperings. She speaks in our
spirit.

Her voice yields to our silences like a faint light that quivers in fear of the
shadow."

But the restless say, "We have heard her shouting among the mountains,

And with her cries came the sound of hoofs, and the beating of wings and the
roaring of lions."

---

## Notes

- **Writer:** Kahlil Gibran, the Lebanese-American poet and philosopher.
- **Country:** Lebanon (writing in English, from the United States).
- **Era:** Early 20th century; The Prophet was published in 1923.
- **Theme:** Beauty as many things at once — kind and gentle, mighty and dread, soft and restless, a thing that cannot be fixed in a single definition.
- **Contrast:** Where Wilde's Dorian Gray (prose1) shows beauty corrupted by worship, Gibran's prose shows beauty as a living mystery that cannot be possessed. Together they show beauty as both a gift and a danger.
